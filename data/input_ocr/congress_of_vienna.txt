The Congress of Vienna was convened in 1814 to reconstruct Europe after the Napoleonic Wars.
Prince Klemens von Metternich, the Austrian foreign minister, played a central role in the
negotiations. The Congress was attended by representatives from Austria, Prussia, Russia,
and Great Britain.

Tsar Alexander I of Russia sought to expand Russian influence across the continent. The
resulting Concert of Europe established a balance of power that lasted for decades. Baron
Karl vom Stein, a Prussian statesman, also participated in early discussions but died
before the Congress concluded.

The Treaty of Paris was signed on May 30, 1814, preceding the Congress. Metternich later
became the dominant figure in European diplomacy, championing conservatism against
nationalist and liberal movements.

In 1815, Napoleon escaped from Elba and returned to France, triggering the Hundred Days.
The Seventh Coalition, led by the Duke of Wellington and Prussian Field Marshal Gebhard
Leberecht von Blucher, defeated Napoleon at Waterloo on June 18, 1815. This final defeat
led to Napoleon's exile on Saint Helena, where he died in 1821.

The Holy Alliance, proposed by Alexander I in 1815, sought to unite the Christian monarchs
of Europe. Metternich viewed it as a useful tool for suppressing revolutionary movements
but privately considered Alexander's idealism impractical.
