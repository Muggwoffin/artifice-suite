Moscow, April 22, 1935
Kusmetzki Most 9, District 9

Dear Dr. Finkelstein,

Thank you for your letter. However, this presents some difficulties. If Karin is a member of the party, she must first obtain permission from the relevant authorities before traveling here. Should she wish to work here, she will be required to do so under the same conditions as Russian specialists – that is, without the usual benefits afforded to foreign experts. The question of accommodation is particularly challenging. During the summer months, it may still be possible, as many residents of the estate rent out their rooms in Moscow at this time. However, during the winter, this becomes significantly more difficult. Furthermore, such a room would cost approximately 100-150 rubles, which would leave her with very little income. She would then have to live on an estate, which is considerably cheaper, but also much more demanding due to the constant travel and work, and secondly, we simply do not have space. We currently share two rooms between six men. We will certainly assist her in any way that we can.

Do you happen to have Professor Nathanson's address? Are you familiar with the production of furfural from the distillation of wood? If so, please let me know what literature is available on this subject.

With best regards,
Your old friend,
(sd) K. Ceul Roung (or Keunz)
