Here he states, he found lodgings at No. 54, Lillington Street, Victoria, S.W., and after earning a few pounds drawing cartoons for "Everybodies Weekly", he left and went to live with Rose FYLEMAN, an American authoress of No. 10, Luxborough House, Luxborough Street, W.1., where he wrote a book entitled "Kingdom of Rags", using the name "John OLDAY". FYLEMAN translated the work for him but after two months he and she quarrelled and he took a small room at No. 51, Greek Street, Soho. The book was published by Jarrolds of London and sold 500 copies - he received an advance royalty of £50 but nothing since.

In February 1939 he returned to Amsterdam and later Antwerp where he states he made contact with German refugees who hate the Nazi regime as much as he does. He declines to give the names or other particulars of these refugees but was referred by them to Karl GROEHL @ FRIEDBERG of Rue Barrault 7, Paris Xlll, whom he described as a member of the central committee of the communist party. GROEHL was approached by OLDAG to work for the overthrow of Hitlerism and OLDAG states that he was prepared to commit acts of sabotage for this purpose. He states that GROEHL promised him supplies of a colourless liquid with strong acid properties for sabotage in munition factories, docks and warehouses; a supply of fountain pens which explode when unscrewed and a quantity of suit-cases which explode upon opening. OLDAG states that whenever he pressed GROEHL for these explosives the latter always put him off with some excuse such as "that the time was not opportune". GROEHL ultimately sent OLDAG to Switzerland with the promise to supply him with a quantity of the acid. This he failed to do and after repeated requests he stated that he would forward the formula. This also GROEHL refused to do and OLDAG then decided to return to Paris, but upon arriving at Geneva after the outbreak of war he was entrained for London as a refugee.
