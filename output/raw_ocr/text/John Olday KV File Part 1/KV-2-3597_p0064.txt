F.1.

As requested, I have examined the situation in each of the eleven cases (now numbered for more convenient reference) in the attached B.M.

1. W. STRAUSS. We have no trace. I suggest investigation by F.1.a.

2. H. QUASNITSCHKA. There are traces of this man in L.260/3022 and S.F.53/8/19(559). They contain, however, no Internal Security element and I consider this case more one for Dy.D.b. that F.1.a.

3. R. MUTZKE. This man is being investigated by F.1.a. in L.260/2552. His British born wife married him after the outbreak of war and last year Special Branch reported that there was "good reason to suspect that they were actually pro-Nazi". (L.260/2552 4a, last paragraph.) There is no recent information against them and pending the receipt of an up to date report from the Police, the C.O. has not yet been communicated with. I suggest that this case should be pursued by F.1.a.

4. O. CURA. We have no trace. I suggest that this is a case for Dy.D.b.

5. C. WINTER. We have no trace. I suggest that this is a case for F.1.a.

6. R.W. DITTRICH. We have no trace. I suggest that this is case for Dy.D.b.

7. L. RETTNER. This man, described as a butcher, born in London of German parents, appears not to be identical with L. Rettner, a Swiss Pencil Company Director, who has been dealt with by Mr. Craufurd's Section, in L.293/44. On this assumption, there appears to be no Internal Security element in the case which should be passed to Dy.D.b.

8. D.R. LANZER. We have no trace. I do not consider this a case for F.1.a. I doubt whether it is a case for anyone.

9. W. OLDAG. This man is already being actively investigated by F.1.c. in P.F.45000. He was an active member of the Y.C.L. in Germany, i.e., anti-Nazi. His case is obviously not a serious one. As the B.M. contains a more up to date location of this man than F.1.c. have, their attention might be drawn to this.

10. H.A. STEINBICHLER. This man was investigated in P.F.46745 between 1938 and 1941 on suspicion of espionage. On 3.1.41. B.22. decided that "there now appears to be no special reason to suspect him from the M.I.5. point of view". Since then information has been received from Western Command H.Q. that he has served in the German Army, that his sympathies are entirely for Germany and that he says he refuses to serve in the British Army. I consider that this case should be taken up by F.1.a.

11. A. ARMANI. This man has trace s in 402391. He has been reported by Western Command H.Q. as stating that he is an Italian, his entire sympathy is with the Italian nation, he has no wish to serve in the British Army and has refused to don uniform. His younger brother, Bruno, has adopted a similar attitude. This seems a borderline case between Internal and Military Security and on the whole I consider it rather more the latter.

With regard to the suggestion of discharge and internment, it seems possible that is cases (10) and (11) - STEINBICHLER and ARMANI - investigation may produce sufficient substantiation of the reports to justify this. In the other cases, it seems unlikely that either discharge or internment will be justified on Internal Security grounds.

In addition to the action suggested above, 'C' Branch may well be interested in these instances of incorrect posting.

Owing to the short time available, it has not been possible to have extracts made from the B.M., since photographed.

Sgd: R.G. Micklethwait.
