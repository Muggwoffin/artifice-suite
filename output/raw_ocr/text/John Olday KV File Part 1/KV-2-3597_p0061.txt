Extracted from A.G.14A/5416 re Past History of 11 O.Rs in Pioneer Corps.

The first point to make quite clear is that these 11 men are British subjects of dual nationality (all British - enemy). In my view the next step is for the War Office to make up its mind whether these men should be allowed to continue to serve in view of their alleged loyalty to the enemy cause. In this connection it must be clearly established whether the individual has openly stated or otherwise indicated the direction in which his loyalty lies, or whether the conclusion has been reached by the C.O. merely on an examination of the man's history and parentage, etc.

I would advise that the War Office should not concern itself too much about the question of whether the man is to be interned; that is a matter for the Home Office to deal with at a later stage. The immediate problem for the War Office is to decide whether the man should be discharged from the Army.

As soon as a decision has been reached that he should be discharged M.I.5 should be notified with the fullest particulars as to reasons. M.I.5 and the Home Office will then get together and consider whether or not there is a case for internment. If the solution is internment the matter ends. If the Home Office decision is against internment it is then for the Ministry of Labour to find suitable employment for the individual.

Enclosed at 5A are some observations by M.I.5 on the individuals concerned.

It is recommended that agreement should be reached on this procedure between A.G, Home Office and M.I.5.

D.M.I.
26.11.41.
