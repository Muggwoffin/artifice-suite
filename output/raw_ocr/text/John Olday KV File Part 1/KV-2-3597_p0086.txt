At this point OLDAG became evasive and refused to answer any questions regarding either his own or his wife's activities either politically or in regard to their business associates.

He left England on 18th February, 1959, returning to Amsterdam, and later toured Belgium, France and Switzerland, returning to Paris where he met a number of German political refugees and a nameless organisation was formed, the sole object of which would appear to be the destruction, by means of sabotage, of points of military importance in Germany. The principal of this group, he stated, was a German named Karl GROEHL or FRIEDBERG of 7, Rue Barrault, Paris, XIII, which address is also the headquarters of the organisation.

OLDAG stated that he had spent a great deal of time working on behalf of the above mentioned group, particularly during the past three months, when he had been travelling to and from Switzerland, Germany and France, meeting the various contacts in Basle, Geneva, Freiberg and other border towns on the Swiss-German frontier. He was now in England to propose to the military authorities here that a number of travelling trunks, packed with explosives - made up in the style adopted by the I.R.A. - should be sent to high German officials. OLDAG stated that his organisation would undertake to convey the trunks over the frontiers and see that 7 trunks so composed should find their mark on payment by the British of 7,000 Belgian francs. With this object in view he had arrived in London from Geneva on a special refugee train yesterday, and now had in his possession about 2/6. He gave as his intended address 54, Lillington Street, Victoria, but added that he had no baggage there apart from his toilet requisites.
