SUBJECT: John William Oldag.

Ref: SO/R/39 (147).

To: The Security Officer, London Area.

Sir,

I have to report that on 16th September I reported as ordered to the R.T.O. at Waterloo Station, at 3.25 p.m. The R.T.O. told me that he wished me to interrogate a German-speaking man who had entered this country with a British passport and whose account of himself was not satisfactory. The man claimed to be a British subject by reason of being born in London, and produced documents purporting to relate to himself, the genuineness of which could not be determined by inspection. The R.T.O.'s suspicions were based on the fact that the man knew nothing at all about Freiburg, where he said he had recently been, and I discovered also that he knew very little about Hamburg, where he said he was educated. His story was that he was able to organize sabotage in Germany if the British Government would supply the necessary explosives. His story and his inconsistencies were sufficient to justify further investigation, whether he were lying or truthful, and I accordingly reported the facts to my Officer, with the result that two Officers from the S.B. of Scotland Yard were sent to interrogate him, which they did most thoroughly. I assisted at this meeting until 8.0 p.m., when it was suggested that I need no longer stay, although the interrogation was not yet concluded.

The impression I gathered so far was that there was no prima facie case for detaining the man, although verification of the many vague, incomplete and doubtful statements made by the man would no doubt prove that he was in every way an 'undesirable alien', as indeed he appeared to be by inspection. His alleged political leanings, his associates, and his mode of life, and his record - he was in prison for embezzlement - pointed in the same direction.

G. Friedhoffer
Sgt.
C. M. Police.

London.
17th September, 1939.
