You will find that policy on the discharge or retention of soldiers of this type has been clarified since the date of your visit to No. 12 Training Centre, Pioneer Corps, in S.F.57/2/5. This has been done in the light of an agreement that has been arrived at with the Ministry of Labour whereby soldiers who are discharged from the Army on security grounds, whom it is not possible or desirable to intern, may be drafted to useful forms of civilian labour such as afforestation or drainage under the Ministry of Labour. In deciding whether or not a particular soldier is to be discharged, the point of view of the Army is now the paramount consideration to be borne in mind, and in view of this, and of the information which appears on this file, I feel strongly in favour of:-

The undermentioned being retained:
No. 13095115  Pte. OLDAG, W.

Incidentally observation in the Pioneer Corps is only resorted to in very special circumstances from the military security point of view because generally speaking it has seldom proved to be a satisfactory method of ascertaining a soldier's sympathies or loyalty.

I would suggest that, where they do not exist already, separate files should be made for each of the men whom you have interviewed and further that a copy of each report you have made should be put on the appropriate file.

Unfortunately this matter is extremely urgent as although the War Office files were marked to M.I.5. on 23.3.42, they only reached me from C.2. on 13.4.42, and even then without our own files. This delay was apparently due to C. waiting for policy to be clarified which has now been done. In the circumstances it would be preferable to avoid having to send these files down to Oxford again and if you were to reply to the War Office minute yourself, and perhaps you will have a word with C.2./D. (C. & D.) should you think fit.
