SUBJECT Arthur William or John OLDAG or OLDAY and wife Hilda.
Reference to Papers 320/EPA/517. 501/MP/5258.
23rd day of July 1940.
With reference to letter (MA/DL), dated 1.7.40. from Arram, Fairfield and Co., Solicitors, 11, Queen Street, Bank, E.C.4., regarding John OLDAY, of 54, Lillington Street, S.W.1:-
Enquiries show that this man is identical with Arthur William or John OLDAG (or OLDAY), of 54, Lillington Street, S.W.1. who is the subject of Special Branch file No. 320/EPA/517 and who is already known to M.I.5.
OLDAY is at present without employment although he claims that he is making every endeavour to obtain work. He spends a great deal of his time writing short articles and stories with a view to having them accepted by periodicals and newspapers. For the time being he is living on the charity of his landlady at the above address.
He still adheres to the story told by him to Special Branch officers when he arrived in this country on 16th September last. With regard to the mention in informant's letter of OLDAY's connection with the International Friendship League - he states that he has attended meetings of that organisation and was approached by them to give lectures on Germany. The organisation is subject of Special Branch file No. 320/NAZ/492 and is regarded as a genuine peace organisation.
Youths of all nationalities belong to the International Friendship League, which claims to be a non-political organisation. It was, before the war, the aim of the organisation to arrange meetings between the youth of various nations and to foster their good relations. However, since the war began, refugees of all nationalities have been encouraged to join and take part in their meetings and social functions. At these functions, German folk songs are sung to a large extent owing to a large preponderance in the audiences of German speaking refugees.
As far as the allegation that OLDAY teaches young Irishmen German folk songs is concerned - these are persons whom he has met at International Friendship League meetings.
OLDAY is violently anti-Nazi, as his past history shows, and there seems little likelihood that he would take part in any activities which would aid the Nazis in their drive against this country. On the other hand, since he has, in the past, been associated with political organisations which advocate violence and sabotage, should circumstances change in this country he may become a possible source of danger.
