Tell T & W that I haven't sort out Plevinsky, because I was solemnly warned of him. I haven't yet investigated the matter, but I mean to. Parowan I met soon after arrival, & to be quite frank, though I haven't so far spoken further for him. Something about him makes me uneasy. I can't exactly define it, but I'm not exactly comfortable in his company. Maybe you will see; he is sending a note to you. By the way, H.P. felt the same about it quite independently. I have been to call on Maumee's mother & sister. The latter I thought uncannily like Andrew, even to her voice. I admit she's got a little more hair; but otherwise she's the spurt of him. His mother has been rather ill, & has just come into the country.

Do you know Spanski Hill, where the Russian Moscow bus & all that? It's becoming a great institution for universal mass training. All the Russian youth, male & female, foregather there and let themselves go. The idea is to direct their high spirits towards the universal needs of Russia—in other words, to make them fit to stand up against the world. They have erected an enormous stadium for mass displays, while physical developing apparatus (or what apparatus) are put in the way of the youth in quest of sport and amusement. It would be a great place for you. You could get cricket, & to speak—because they really play football—without side-whiskers. I can't think of a suitable word to describe the Boston-Clinton crowd. But they sound pretty awful. Still the cricket's good, so if I come you I would put up with the side-whiskers.

My love to all in the Dept, excluding T & W. Write again soon.
