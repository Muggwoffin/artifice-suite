HARWICH.

HODANN. Max.

NATIONALITY. German. OCCUPATION. Doctor of Medicine.
SEX. Male. BORN. Neisse 30-8-94.
PASSPORT. 48/Rev/28/31. Berlin 24-2-31.
PROPOSED ADDRESS IN U.K. c/o Max Beer, 180 Regents Park Rd, London, N.W.,

Arrived on ms "Esbjerg" from Esbjerg 17-3-35.

Stated that he intended staying 3-4 weeks studying social conditions in U.K. Would stay with his friend Max BEER at above address. Had about £10 in his possession.

Alien is a political refugee from Germany, and, since November 1934, has been living in Norway. Has permission to remain in that country until 10th August 1935, and stated that he hoped to stay there indefinitely. Apparently no longer practises as Medical Doctor and writing articles for newspapers on social questions is his sole means of livelihood. He is a member of the Danish P.E.N. Club and is authorised correspondent of Danish newspaper "Arbeiderbladet". He produced newspaper cuttings showing that his articles had been published in various European countries. He is interested in eugenics and has written articles on birth control. His ideas on social questions appear to be Communist rather than Socialist.

Max BEER, the friend whom he intended visiting, is a German, resident in U.K. for the last two years, and is also a writer on social matters. In a letter to HODANN, BEER refers to a book he (BEER) has just completed, the title being "50 years of Socialism". He draws the attention of HODANN to one chapter in particular - "Talks with Lenin". Alien was not in possession of a letter of invitation from BEER, and after his refusal HODANN did not appear to wish to get in touch with BEER.

HODANN was accompanied by Mrs LINDBOK-NILSON and her child. (See report H/H.11599). Both aliens spent some time during 1934 in Palestine, collaborating in writing a book "Jews are Coming Home". Mrs LINDBOK-NILSON is now engaged in writing a biography of Dr Nansen and again HODANN is collaborating. She stated that HODANN did not wish to return to Norway as he had no work there.

HODANN expected to obtain more money from newspapers (mainly foreign) for the articles which he would write. The case was referred to H.M.I. who directed refusal of leave to land under Art. 1(1) and 1(3)a. Subsequently particulars
