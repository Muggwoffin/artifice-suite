Extract from H.O. file F.825.

Conditional Landing.

Port of Harwich. Date of landing 5.1.33.
Name. Alexis FINKELSTEIN.
Nationality. German. Occupation. Chemist.
Date of birth 25.1.77. Place of birth Leipzig.
Passport No.251 - Bernberg - 27.10.32.
Address to which proceeding Kings Farm, Little Shelford, Cambs.

Remarks. Alien, who is obviously a chemist of considerable standing, stated that he has come for a consultation with his friend Dr. BAYON and desired to stay three weeks.
Dr. BAYON, who is said to be a professor at the Molteno Institute for research in Parasitology at Cambridge University is connected with the firm Cellulose Compositions Co.Ltd., of Trumpington, Cambs. Letters between alien and Dr. BAYON were produced which showed that experiments have been made in the reaction for industrial manufacture of plaster materials and that alien's services are required for improving the process.
The process is used by Cellulose Compositions Co. in the manufacture of accumulator lids.
Alien's fare together with £15 for expenses, had been forwarded to him by Dr. BAYON.
Alien stated that he is a consulting chemist and that his services have been called for by many large industrial firms.
As there is a possibility that his services may be retained by the above named firm for an indefinite period he was granted leave to land for one month and instructed that application for any required extension should be made to the H.O.
FINKELSTEIN mentioned that should his services not be required beyond the three weeks he would endeavour to come to an arrangement whereby he would represent the process in Germany.

21.8.33. Arrived Harwich. Conditionally landed for one month.

He produced further correspondence from Dr. BAYON, a director of Cellulose Compositions Co.Ltd., which showed that he has again come to carry out similar research work for them. Alien stated that he wished to remain for a period of six months as there was a great deal more work to be done than on the last occasion.
He stated that the firm would not pay him a regular salary but would, he thought, pay him his out of pocket expenses, although no definite arrangement had been made by him with the firm. From the tone of the letters produced, it would appear that alien is on friendly terms with Dr. BAYON.
FINKELSTEIN was in possession of the usual sum of 200 German marks.
