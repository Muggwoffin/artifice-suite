Notes on fps. at 12a.

Edward R. ELKAN.

We have the following reference to one ELKAN in a letter to Dr. GEMMECKE at 5, Little Russell Street, from his wife in Krefeld, dated 16.8.30.:

"Elkan has been here 8 days. He has been in Antwerp and Luttich and, so he says, Paris......."

She went on to say that ELKAN proposed "in his boastful way" to go to England the following year.

Note. 5, Little Russell Street is a boarding house run by a Mrs. HEUGH who formerly figured on the M.I.5. Black List. In 1930 the boarding house was considered to be a recognised lodging place for Soviet agents visiting this country.

Dr. Edgar OBERMER.

1. On 21.3.16. one Edgar OBERMER - American, a suspect - was reported to have arrived at Southampton from Havre. He was coming from Berne and said he had been in Lausanne for his health. He was proceeding to 1, Victoria Square, London and then to "Lyndora", Shoreham.

Fps. of the latter address show that it was the address of one Leonard Francis LYNDHURST (formerly Francis Lyndhurst SCHMITZ), a British born subject. His father was a Frenchman of German extraction.

In 1915 this man was Managing Director of The Searchlight Film, Broadway, Co.Ltd. He was financed by one Seymour OBERMER, an American born in Tennessee - a "manufacturer's agent" - with an office at 18, rue Brunel, Paris - who, before the War supplied motor car accessories.

At this time LYNDHURST was reported to have resided in Shoreham for some years and had a film studio there. Some time during 1915 OBERMER's son, who "spoke fluent German but had no experience in cinema work" was put in charge of Cinema production at Shoreham.

In June 1915 LYNDHURST entered into partnership with Seymour OBERMER. In the autumn of that year the latter left for Italy with his son who was ill and offered LYNDHURST easy terms to buy him out of business.

In 1917 OBERMER's address was La Thioleyre, Bethusy, Lausanne.

In August 1917 one Henry Charles MINTER, agent for Seymour OBERMER (motor accessories) applied in Rome for a passport to go to Lausanne to visit OBERMER. He is said to have created an unfavourable impression.

In February 1918, Seymour OBERMER, who was in London, stated that his wife and son were at 11, Grand Chêne, Lausanne.
