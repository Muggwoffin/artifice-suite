STOKKE
Tveretskaya 36
Moscow

September 21, 1933.

Dear Hig,

A thousand thanks for your lovely long letter. I have been meaning to write to you every day for the last two or three weeks, but somehow one never does what one intends to in Moscow. I have even let my Russian slip the last few weeks while Trilby is here—but of course I mean to make up for it! Moscow is a deadly place for just drifting—time just goes without your noticing it. There are so many people about, and you talk and drink tea and go on talking, and then you find the day has gone.

I have been saying to myself ever since I've been here, when 'honk' goes, I will really settle down to work. But another 'honk' has always turned up. However, I really think Trilby will be the last.

Andrew arrived a week ago, and we have had some long talks together. Did you hear the story how I married him to Violet Lansbury; at least, some old gossip here told me that they had been married, and I, being a true woman, passed on the information to all those whom I thought would be interested.

I then received a terse note from Andrew informing me that he was not married to Violet Lansbury, that he had no intention of marrying her, and that he didn't believe that communists ought to marry at the present time! It was all very unfortunate, but he took it very well when I met him.

Andrew tells me that you are now definitely installed in Arco's. I'm awfully glad—I never really believed that you would go back to Australia, but I was rather worried about your finding another job immediately. It's awful to think that the old Department is all broken up. It was a jolly crowd, don't you think? We were all so much nicer than any other fraternity—I feel almost sentimental about it.
