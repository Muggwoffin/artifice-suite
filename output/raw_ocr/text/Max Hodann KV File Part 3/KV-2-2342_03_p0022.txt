Since the inauguration of the Settlement a great deal has been done in the way of developing Unemployed Clubs in the Rhondda.

A large instructional Poultry Farm was established by the Settlement in 1931 and as a result a number of unemployed men received training and eventually received financial assistance to set out with poultry farms on their own account, in various parts.

This instructional farm has now been disbanded since 1934.

The Settlement has given considerable help in organizing migratory schemes. Allotment holders were encouraged to form associations and by this means they were enabled, through the Society of Friends, to obtain seeds, garden tools etc. at cost price. A Fruit and Vegetable Show is held annually under the auspices of the Settlement.

They have established in all about 25 Unemployed Clubs in the Rhondda where unemployed men are taken on at a membership of 1d per week. Employed men who wish to become members are asked to pay 2d per week.

The initial cost of establishing these Clubs is borne by the Settlement and later each club is placed on a self governing basis, each having its own committee, secretary & treasurer.

They are instructed in Cabinet making, bootrepairing etc. Each Club has its football team, hold physical training classes, Library, and during the winter months have lectures and lantern shows.

They also have a wireless set and an organised 'Listeners Group'.

There are games and where sufficient interest is shown a choir is formed.
