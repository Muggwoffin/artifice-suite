CONFIDENTIAL.

FROM: H.
Zurich.
Switzerland.

8.10.41.

TO: W. Eichler,
24 Mandeville Rise,
Welwyn Garden City.

Letter in German from which extracts are taken.

"We will try to find out something about the people for whom you are enquiring and about whom nothing has ever been heard so far. Some time ago I had LEONHARDT's address and will try and trace it again. If he is in VERNET, all efforts to get him out will be in vain. Tremendous exertions have been made on behalf of several people there, for many steady and valuable people are in there. Among them the singer, Fritz BUSCH, also BOEGLER and FRITZ LAMM and many others. SIMONNE has already put a great deal of work and time into attempts to help, but the people who are interned there are the ones... who are considered, probably quite unjustifiably, particularly dangerous. A large number of Italians and Spaniards were there too. The Italians have now been sent back to Italy. Many Spaniards have been extradited, others sent to work in places in Occupied France which the R.A.F. bombards, - Germans are likewise transferred. Many were confronted with the question - were they prepared to return to Germany? If they declined they were placed in special custody and one day fetched away by the Gestapo. I expect you have recently received the Camp Report. Truly the French right of asylum is a sorry chapter! Many Frenchmen are profoundly ashamed and agitated. Many try by their personal efforts to alleviate the lot of the internees, - for instance "Equipes Sociales" work on hygienic grounds. But they cannot accomplish much."

"PETER GAUNT came in and we had an interesting evening. He told us a lot about England, and we got some suggestions from him as to how we could help you for the BBC. Although we have not known him long we like him - I feel partly because he seems to us a bridge to you. Pity that you did not know of his journey beforehand then the connection would have been more direct. Now he will be able to tell you about us, and also explain a few cautionary measures we have in mind in case the Nazis establish posta, l-control in Spain or in Portugal. One can never tell what they will decide to do, especially when things get worse for them. It would be bad if signed letters just happened to be on their way whose contents one did not wish the Nazis to know about.

Naturally I have not forgotten KLEINBERG..... In these days ins CZEcho-Slovakia I am always thinking of him. He was too old to get away, and perhaps he may be - if he has not already been - one of HEYDRICH's victims.
