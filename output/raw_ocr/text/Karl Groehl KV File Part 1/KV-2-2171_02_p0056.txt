OTTEN then told me of an incident that occurred last Thursday about noon, when he found himself being followed by a thickset medium height man wearing spectacles, a nondescript suit, and a blue felt hat of a type that struck him as Continental. As he believed his house (No. 19 Oppidans Rd.) had been watched for some time, he deliberately dawdled, thus causing the tailer some embarrassment, and eventually the latter disappeared into No. 5 Oppidans Rd. He reappeared again shortly afterwards, and this time OTTEN accosted him, thus causing further embarrassment. The man, who spoke English with a perfect accent, denied accusations of molestation, but continued to tail OTTEN, and was shortly joined by another man. They hung on for a little while, but eventually gave it up and went back to No. 5. This house, OTTEN says, is supposed to be a students' hostel of sorts, and there is a lot of singing there at night. OTTEN thinks that most of the lodgers are Mosleyites, but believes that it is run under Gestapo auspices and that the lodgers can be used by the Gestapo without realising for what purpose. He told me that he had reported the incident to the police, and was anxious to know whether as an Englishman presumably acquainted to some extent with police procedure I could tell him whether the police could do anything. I replied that as far as I could see, no actual offence had been committed, but that I was not conversant with such things. As he was particularly anxious to help put a stop to any Nazi activities, he said he was concerned because there was an anti-aircraft battery on Primrose Hill, practically under the nose of these people. That, I maintained, would be a pure coincidence, and in any case anti-aircraft batteries could hardly be concealed. Nevertheless, you may think it worth while looking into the affairs of No. 5."
