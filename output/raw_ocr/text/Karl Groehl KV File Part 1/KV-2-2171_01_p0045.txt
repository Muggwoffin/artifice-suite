METROPOLITAN POLICE.
SPECIAL BRANCH,
Metropolitan Police,
Scotland House,
21st day of March, 1931.

With reference to M.I.5 enquiry, RL.451/362/B4.c., of 16th December, 1940, asking for any possible information concerning Samuel URIEWICZ's suspected contact with the Soviet Embassy.

It is extremely probable that URIEWICZ is in close touch with the Soviet Embassy, although he endeavours to create the impression that he is opposed to the Stalin regime. He was, however, a friend of the present Russian Ambassador, M. MAISKY, when they were both in Berlin in 1923, and there is every reason to believe that the acquaintanceship has been maintained. It is possibly significant that URIEWICZ has been heard to say that he did not believe M. Maisky to be a true follower of Stalin.

There are many possible contacts which URIEWICZ may have, as his main connections are with left wing sympathisers. At the present time he is endeavouring to establish a central anti-fascist organisation of an international character. His view is that the defeat of Hitler and Mussolini is not sufficient, and he maintains that the re-establishment of the old governments will only result in the continuance of fascism under another guise. In my opinion the scheme is an organisation to establish communism in European countries, but it is represented as an anti-fascist scheme in order to secure the support of prominent socialists.

An associate of URIEWICZ is Frank or Leo ZEITLIN, of Manor House, Dorchester-on-Thames, Oxon., and late of Imperial House, 80-86, Regent Street, W.1., where he used to occupy an office as "Sinclair Textiles". During the course of enquiries it was noticed that John McGOVERN, M.P., accompanied by Saul HARRIS, from the Central Hotel, Glasgow, frequently visited the Piccadilly Hotel, W. On these occasions McGovern was invariably seen with ZEITLIN, and, as URIEWICZ has been heard to say he could get the support of McGovern but he is not powerful enough to sponsor his anti-fascist scheme, it is very probable that ZEITLIN is the 'go-between' in this case.

URIEWICZ also has an interest in the P.S.T. Trading Co. Ltd., manufacturers of textiles, of 80-86, Regent Street, W.1., the exact nature of which has not so far been established, although this is the same address as that used by ZEITLIN mentioned above. The directors of this firm are Robert James FIFER, 19, Elms Road, S.W.4, and Michael TIKOTSKY, Polish Former Russian, 25, Hillcrest Court, N.W.2. The shareholders include Joseph SAGALL, formerly SAGALOVITCH, Russian, 20, Hillcrest Court, N.W.2, and subject of Special Branch report of 24th July, 1940, (320/EPA/9508) and West Hampstead police report of 12th December, 1940, (79/S/6571).

SAGALL, born Vilna, Russia, 2nd December, 1905, was educated in Moscow until 1923, when he moved to Danzig.
