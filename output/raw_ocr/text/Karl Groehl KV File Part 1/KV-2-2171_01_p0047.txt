URIWICZ now appears to be working with two other Polish subjects, one of whom is a Polish Government servant, and this group is actively contacting aliens who have money freely available abroad. They have recently made overtures to the Polish Government for the sale of foreign currency abroad, and it is undoubtedly these activities which have come to the notice of the Bank of England and which are referred to in the Bank's enquiry of 24th February, 1941, on file 320/MPA/8411.

This interim report is submitted to indicate how extensive are Uricwicz's activities in this country, and to answer M.I.5's enquiry. Enquiries are being continued to establish the identity of Uricwicz's accomplices and the extent of his financial operations. Although he may be dealing in foreign currency solely for his own personal gain this seems unlikely and he too may wish to use currency abroad for propaganda for himself or another foreign government.

Samuel URIKVICZ has been a political intriguer since he was a boy. He is extremely cunning and has numerous contacts, most of whom are claimed as business acquaintances. Nevertheless, he cannot show that he is engaged in any legitimate occupation although he is able to live in considerable comfort at the Piccadilly Hotel. As far as can be ascertained he has no bank account and always pays his bills in cash. From every aspect his activities are inimical to the best interests of this country, and he appears to be a fit subject for internment.

With reference to the offences committed by Dipa X HONKESSTAJN and Samuel URIKVICZ, contrary to Section 18 of the Aliens Order, 1920; as sub-section (8) of section 18 stipulates that proceedings for false representation must be made within two months of the discovery of the offence, it is no longer possible to prosecute for these offences.
