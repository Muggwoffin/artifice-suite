I asked OTTEN why his crowd had made no attempt to touch British politicians of anti-Nazi sentiments, and he told me this had been done, but with no success. The answers generally obtained were to the effect that all funds must be conserved for a forthcoming general election, but OTTEN thinks the people approached were really afraid of Communism in Germany if Hitler was overthrown. He thinks their fears are unnecessary, and that if these movements could be given a hint from authoritative British sources that neither Bolshevism nor the Hohenzollerns would be tolerated in Germany by Britain, then all concerned would be ready to work for a democratic republic.

Hardly a week goes by without GROEHL being visited in Paris by agents of the civil anti-Hitler movement, all of whom are business or professional men. OTTEN told me that the last two Mr. SMITH letters contained political news of the movement in addition to matter concerning secret aerodromes, and that the political news is passed on to Czecho-Slovakia along with the other stuff. He dwelt on the need for accelerating progress, because he is convinced that Germany means to attack soon; the south east parts of Germany are now fully mobilised and ready for action. Also Germany has been very successful in her wheat deals and has bought up the export produce of Rumania and Hungary for the next ten years.

OTTEN gave me a couple of sheets dealing with the present strength of the Hitler Youth for military purposes. He said this material had never yet been published, but if I could get any paper - local or national - to publish it, it might do good. He also told me that Germany was now recalling all technicians from England, which he considered ominous. He has spoken with several who have been ordered to pack up and knows one or two who have made up their minds to disobey.
