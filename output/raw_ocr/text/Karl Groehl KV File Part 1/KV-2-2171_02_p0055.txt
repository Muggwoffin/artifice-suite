B.4b.

re Carl OTTEN group:

The following report has been received from M/S:-

"Carl OTTEN came down to see me yesterday, but appeared to be more interested in his literary affairs than in the imminence of the crisis.

Nevertheless, he told me that he had been doing political work for the last week or so, and had had interviews with the War Office and Air Ministry in connection with discoveries he had made and information which had come to hand. Presumably he refers to information passed on to him by the other Karl (GROEHL) in Paris. He says he hopes the latter will get through for a visit next Saturday.

GROEHL, said OTTEN, had at last been able to secure the list of all the stations for all German army units, which he said was a very secret affair. I do not know the correct name for this, but he described it as the confidential documents which tells where every regiment, battery, battalion, air squadron etc. is stationed. I mention this as a check up which may be useful in case your friends have not received this information from GROEHL indirectly.

OTTEN also told me that sabotage is on the increase in Germany, particularly in the aircraft factories, where the workers find it their only means of expressing their dissatisfaction with the policy of their government. A few weeks ago six crashes were due to this form of sabotage.

Carl OTTEN has seen an editor (present or former?) of the "Berliner Tagblatt", who was in London recently. The latter told him that there is considerable disaffection among the younger pilots of the German Air Force, who do not want to get themselves killed for the Sudeten. He also spoke of disaffection among the members of the German aristocracy, whose dislike of the Hitler regime is increasing owing to the intense spying to which they are subjected.

OTTEN claims to be in touch with a German engineer who has been resident in this country for some time and is now working for the Air Ministry - a very gifted man, but he dare not tell me his name. He likewise claims contact with a former captain in the Austrian Army, who told him that the anti-Nazi feeling was on the increase and that the Tyrol is seething with disaffection. The one idea of the peasants there is to get a chance to polish off all the Nazi officials, and they would not mind destroying Vienna in the process, but at present their rage appears to be too inarticulate to count as a factor in the situation.

From other sources he learned that Colonel Beck would like to make common cause with Germany, but dare not undertake anything because he could find no support in Poland. OTTEN prophesied that Beck would be assassinated the day that he tried to start anything and that there would be a revolution in Poland which would give Bolshevism a chance to get going there.

Over 300 Serb and Croat pilots have promised to fly their machines into Czecho-Slovakia and help the Czechs in the event of hostilities. The Yugoslav premier knows this, but dare not interfere. OTTEN also understands that arrangements are completed for allowing Russian troops to march through Rumania to assist the Czechs.
