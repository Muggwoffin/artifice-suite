SECRET.

PF.45000/B4b.  8th October 1939.

Dear Colonel Vivian,

A certain Arthur William OLDAG, a British subject, born in London on 10.4.1905, arrived in the U.K. from Germany on 16.9.39. His wife, Hilda OLDAG nee MEISEL, a student of economics, is believed to be acting as intermediary between members of the Militant Socialist International and other extremist organisations in the U.K. and Germany.

When interrogated by the Police, OLDAG told the following story:— He was the illegitimate son of Anna OLDAG, a German woman, who had taken him to Germany at an early age. He was educated in Kiel and Hamburg. He had been an active member of the Young Communist League in Germany. From 1925 to 1929 he was employed as a cartoonist by the Hamburg paper "Anzeiger".

In 1937, he returned to England and got in touch with Rose FYLEMAN, an American authoress, of 10 Luxborough House, Luxborough Street, W.1. He wrote a book entitled "Kingdom of Rags", using the name of John OLDAY. FYLEMAN translated it for him, and the book was published by JARROLD'S, London. In February 1939, he went to Amsterdam, and later to Antwerp, where he made contact with German anti-Nazi refugees, who referred him to Karl GROEHL, of 7 rue Barrault, Paris XIII, whom he described as a member of the Central Committee of the Communist Party. The latter approached him on the subject of overthrowing Hitlerism by committing acts of sabotage. GROEHL promised to supply him with fountain pens filled with a colourless liquid which explodes on being unscrewed. GROEHL sent him to Switzerland with a promise to supply him with this liquid, but for some unknown reason GROEHL refused to do so, and OLDAG returned to London.

OLDAG stated that his wife, Hilda, was also interested in sabotage plans in Germany.

We have no information about OLDAG, but in view of his alleged associations with GROEHL you may be able to give us some further information about him.

Yours sincerely,

(sd). F.G.D.

Lt. Colonel V. Vivian, C.B.E
S.I.S.
