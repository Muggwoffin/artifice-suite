OLDAG's story of his association with GROEHL cannot be checked here. He adheres to it under close questioning, but admits that he (OLDAG) was the driving force behind the proposal to commit acts of sabotage in Germany, and it may be that GROEHL, after seeing the type of man OLDAG is, sent him on a bogus journey to Basle. OLDAG denies that he is a communist, and states that in Amsterdam and Antwerp he avoided communist circles as they treated him with great reserve and practically accused him of being a Nazi spy.

His story does not ring true, and he impresses one as being a weak type of individual, easily led, gullible and careless in his habits. He is destitute, and maybe he has put up the story to try and gain assistance from the authorities. On the other hand, it is possible that it was the best story he could think up when he found himself back in London under the notice of the authorities, when he anticipated returning to Paris, and if so his recent visit to the Continent may not be altogether innocent of intrigue.
