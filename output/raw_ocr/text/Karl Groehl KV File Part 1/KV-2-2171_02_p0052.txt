B.2a, B.4a.

re Karl OTTEN:

The following report has been received from M/S:-

"Karl OTTEN and his wife came down here for the afternoon to look at houses, but this took up so much time that there was little time for serious conversation. He went to Welwyn last Friday, but likes this place better and is biting at a house. They both seem determined to get out of London as soon as possible, for they are tired of the menage a trois with LEHMANN, and he really wants to devote himself to literature. So it looks as if we shall have to make the best of things in that direction.

What I do not like is the way OTTEN and LEHMANN seem to be mixing themselves up in British politics. Today he seemed to be more interested in things happening this end than across the water. He was somewhat mysterious, but I somehow guess that No. 19 Oppidans Rd., is in very close touch with Winston CHURCHILL, for he spoke of Churchill's coming intervention, and seemed to think that as Chamberlain's only friends in the Cabinet are Simon and Hoare, there will be a movement to get him out of office at the earliest possible moment and that CHURCHILL and Eden will swing a large section of the Conservative Party over to join with Labour and form a new sort of National Government. Then he thinks they could form a united front with France and Russia against Hitler.

He spoke of the Cliveden group being played out and a group connected with the "Daily Telegraph" coming into power.

He began by saying that there would be no war, or if it came it would not last a fortnight, because neither the German army nor the people were behind Hitler; the army might even refuse to march. He has been in contact with a number of German officers in London from whom he says he has information that the fighting strength of the German army has been greatly exaggerated and that it is seething with disaffection. At the same he is in an excited state that makes me think that his desires will influence him to believe what he would like to believe.

He told me that on the night when Chamberlain was in Godesberg last week there had been plans for a complete black-out on a vast scale in Berlin, but that this had to be abandoned because of the discovery or alleged discovery of an army plot to raid government premises during it and shoot all the members of the Nazi Government they could find. According to OTTEN, HIMMLER's influence has been growing in the M.S.D.A.P. but he dare not get rid of Hitler for fear of the people rising up and getting rid of him too. He spoke of Chamberlain and the Cliveden set being anxious to keep Hitler in power, because they were afraid Bolshevism would be the alternative in Germany, which fear OTTEN said was groundless because the Germans would never stand another dictator of any colour. CHURCHILL is working hand in glove with RENAUD in France.

Karl GROEHL in Paris has not received permission yet to come to England, although he now wants to get across. Apparently the approach to British personalities to finance GROEHL's show has been a failure for he told me that he had met with a refusal. Apparently they have now been in touch with the F.O. (perhaps communicating the news from Paris direct instead of through the Czechs), and have been thanked for their endeavours, but they do not seem to be able to get hold of the cash they want to keep things going. I wonder whether a rather wild manner and exaggerated tone on their part have created some prejudice against them."

28.9.38.
