Which remains me, you ought also to send copies of 'Free Unions' (and if you like, copies of those poems which find your approval to the I.W.W. & 'Why'. They might be interested. By the way, have they had 'The Life we live?' They should. They might write again as enthusiastic as they did about 'March to Death.')

About Germany I feel like Heinrich Heine: 'Denk ich an Deutschland in der Nacht, dann bin ich um den Schlaf gebracht.' In other words my heart bleeds. And so do the hearts of all decent men who know or experienced on themselves humiliation, unspeakable suffering and saw death, for the cause of humanity, culture & liberty. (With culture I do not mean civilisation) I listened to a broadcast from London to the P.O.W. camps all over the world. Tucholsky & Osseviersky were of course in a suitable make-up, presented to the German prisoners. Not a word of Minksam. (Thanks to his unmistakable, uncorruptable appearance as an Anarchist.) Certainly T. & O. were pacifists. In this country they would share the fate of all C.O.'s. Their Republicanism, their dreamed off Republic had nothing in common with democracy of our times! I wish I could provide you with quotations from the pens of both these men, who fought & died for something quiet different from the system's of the Allies.
