23rd. July, 1945.

My dear John,

I at last have your correct address from M.L.B. and so hasten to write you a letter of greeting. I have just been typing out those excellent poems of yours which we had had in manuscript; I think some of them should certainly be published in "War Commentary", so that even your present incarceration will not prevent your continued collaboration. Also, we have been digging out some old cartoons of yours for the paper. I think you will be pleased to hear that "Free Unions" is now at the printers, and should be on sale in September - there is a poem of yours in it, of course, with translation. I am very pleased with the contents on the whole. We are going ahead with new plans for the paper, including a new title, and general re-organisation which should mean great improvements; and despite the absence of so many of our friends at the moment we are being helped by several new comrades, and all goes well. John, Vero and the others seem very cheerful, and we hope to see you and them again soon. On the whole I am quite encouraged by the general position: our friends in Glasgow are achieving enormous success, and provide an excellent model for the rest of the country. I hope to spend a few days in Glasgow soon, and will give them your greetings. Meanwhile, I wish you all the best, and hope that existence proves fairly tolerable for you: you certainly looked marvellously fit when I saw you last!

Very best wishes from

(Signed) Simon Watson TAYLOR.
