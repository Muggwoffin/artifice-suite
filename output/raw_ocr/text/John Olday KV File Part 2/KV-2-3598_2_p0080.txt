Note on the case of Arthur William OLDAG @ JOHN OLDAy
Freedom Press Anarchist.

1. Arthur William OLDAG @ John OLDAy claims to be a British subject having been born in London on 10.4.05, the illegitimate son of a German mother and a Scottish father. He states that he was taken as a baby to New York and at the age of eight to Germany, where he was brought up and obtained various jobs. During the last years of his stay in Germany, he was employed as a journalist and cartoonist but he claims to have been engaged in anti-Nazi activities there, as a result of which he left the country in 1937.

2. OLDAG is reported to look younger than his 39 years, with fair hair, brushed back, blue eyes, and a sallow complexion; he is clean shaven. He is described as 5'11" in height (5'8½" from another source), and is effeminate in appearance. The attached photograph was taken in 1937 when he was issued with passport No. C.34071 by the British Consulate in Hamburg.

3. On arrival in England in 1937, OLDAG found lodgings at 54 Lillington Street, Victoria, London, subsequently living with Rose FYLEMAN, an American authoress, at 10, Luxborough House, Luxborough Street, London, W.1. This woman translated OLDAG's book "Kingdom of Rags," which sold 500 copies and was published by Jarrolds under the pseudonym 'John Olday.' After quarrelling with FYLEMAN, he went to live at 51, Greek Street, Soho. In September, 1938, he married Hilda MEISEL, a Hungarian born in Vienna. She is known to Special Branch, Metropolitan Police, under reference 301/MP/5258 and to us under reference PF.45000. Further particulars and a photograph of her are available, but are not included in this note as she is believed to have parted company with OLDAG shortly after the marriage; there are grounds for suspecting that it was one of convenience in order that she should obtain British nationality.

4. In the following February OLDAG went to the Continent, where he states that in association with anti-Nazi refugees from Germany he was engaged on sabotage of military installations in Germany. Owing to the fact that his associates would not give him the necessary assistance to carry out the acts of violence he contemplated he returned from Geneva to this country as a refugee in September 1939. On his arrival here he put forward plans for acts of sabotage against the Nazis, but they were never put into effect, in April, 1941, 51 Lillington Street, the address at which he was staying, was destroyed by enemy action, and he removed to 49 Upper Montague Street, London, W.1.

5. On 17.7.41. OLDAG was called up for military service in the Army (Pioneer Corps) under No. 13095115. His Army papers give his civilian employment as that of shop assistant, and his religion as atheist. On 27.3.43. he deserted from No. 12 Centre, Pioneer Corps, and his name appeared in Gazette No. 211., of 31.5.43. He is still a deserter.

6. Since his desertion from the Army OLDAG has evidently been in close touch with the Freedom Press, whose headquarters are at 27 Belsize Road, London, N.W.6. and has been working for them. In May, 1943, they published his booklet "The March to Death" by 'John Olday'. This consists of cartoons with captions of a grossly subversive and anti-war nature. The State, the Church and the war are all caricatured, and Mr. Churchill, Marshal Stalin and Hitler are all lumped together as being equally responsible for the sufferings of the World. In the Spring of 1944, the Freedom Press published a further book of cartoons by him entitled "The Life we Live, the Death we Die." These are in a similar style, but contain no captions. Owing to their pictorial form these publications seem likely to be more effective propaganda than written material
