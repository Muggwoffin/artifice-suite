Pte. A.W. Oldag,
Military Detention Bks.
Sowerby Bridge, Yorkshire.

My Dear John,

Many thanks for letter received this morning. It is wonderful how you get so much on to so small a piece of paper. I too have often felt how much stray remarks made by me, may influence the future conduct of others. It is a consolation which we ought not to allow ourselves, only congratulate ourselves on visible achievements, however how small and few. Still it is a pleasant luxury, to imagine the growth of the small planted seed growing till it becomes a force of consequence. Yes I have always said that the British workers need suffering. They generally grievously lack the international outlook. The unsympathetic rude way in which soldiers refer to foreigners, (Wogs, Dagoes, Jerry Bastards, etc.) is particularly offensive and betrays a low mentality. The British in this respect remind me of the man in the famous joke, who takes the visitor round the lunatic asylum. "That man over there, now, he thinks he is Julius Caesar. - and that one, thinks he is Jesus Christ, absurd Sir, isn't it? and that one - ridiculous fellow - imagines he is Napoleon. Why he is mad - because - everyone knows that I am Napoleon??" The British worker laughs at foreigners, at Jews and at Hitlers, and Rosenburgs Herronfolk theory, but at least they know they are God's own chosen people, selected to rule the world. (The British Way and Purposes, as the Army Educational pamphlets are called) of course, this is the result of national pride and subtle ruling class propaganda.

I am glad you like the verses I sent. Here's something I wrote last night, of a more serious nature. It is a Soliloquy of '"One Near Death".

Looking back from these gates I have reached but fear the pass,
I see life not whole or perfect, the clear cut vase.
My youth expected from itself to mould,
But only fragments - some fresh, some stained and old,
A miscellany, course and porcelain,
All broken, mere fragments, useless, and inane.
Collection made with clumsiness and skill,
Just to be swept aside in a dark forgotten heap,
Was it all senseless? like the dreams that fill,
Our empty nights with fleshless joy, and pain?
Now am I sick and cynical and cold,
With disillusion and I shake and weep,
Finding life's argument, even here, is still,
Vain, superficial and not plausible.
I live - for what? I die, and it is done?
Those riddles have spawned religions in reply.
In vain, the smiling Sphynx's lips are stone.
What matter if she speaks? For still I die.

How much these feelings are my own, and how much they are imaginary I am not prepared to say. I think that human philosophy has transcended the potentialities of the race. We think too much. Our thoughts bring some joy but at the bottom it is all sorrow. Animals do not think much, and are content not to ask why? They only seem to think, in fact when they are in pain - the reproachful look of the beaten dog, when the animals eyes become human. The human race is only a stage in the evolution of life, a species that has grown to fast, out grown its strength. Knowledge of too much has devitalized our intellectuals. Too little knowledge represses and keeps back the masses.

I am now walking better. Glad you saw M.L., it would be a pleasant surprise.

Yes, granted that the struggles of a Colonial people are national. They cannot keep being so, but it is useless for a people to aim at revolution while their country is in the grip of a greater foreign power, unless they are in international solidarity of workers, otherwise I can only see two ways for the liberation of colonial peoples. (1) A revolution in the imperial power that controls them, or (11) nationalist liberation after a long war, after which stage revolutionary propaganda could begin.

No more just now, I trust you are in good health. My finger is bleeding a little.

Yours Affec. Ivor.
