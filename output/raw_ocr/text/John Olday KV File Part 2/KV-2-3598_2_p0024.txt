In February 1939 Oldag went to the continent where he states that in association with anti-Nazi refugees, he was engaged in sabotage of Military installations in Germany. Owing to the fact that his associates would not give him the necessary assistance to carry out the acts of violence he contemplated, he returned from Geneva to this country as a refugee in September 1939. On his arrival here he put forward plans for acts of sabotage against the Nazis, but they were never put into effect.

In April 1941, 51, Lillington Street, S.W.1. the address at which he was staying, was destroyed by enemy action, and he removed to 49, Upper Montague Street, London, W.1.

On 17th July 1941, Oldag was called up for service in the Army (Pioneer Corps) and was allotted No. 13095115. His Army papers give his civilian occupation as that of a shop assistant and his religion as atheist. He deserted from No. 12, Centre Pioneer Corps on 27th March, 1943, and is still a deserter.

Since his desertion from the Army Oldag has evidently been in close touch with the Freedom Press, whose headquarters are at 27, Belsize Road, London, N.W.6. and has been working for them. In May, 1943, they published his booklet, 'The March to Death', by John Olday. This consists of cartoons with captions of a grossly subversive and anti-war nature.

In the Spring of 1944, the Freedom Press published a further book of cartoons by him entitled 'The Life We Live, The Death We Die'. These are in similar style, but contain no captions. Owing to their pictorial form these publications seem likely to be more effective propaganda than written material.

There is good reason to believe that he is responsible for a number of cartoons which have been appearing in the paper 'War Commentary' during the last 6 months, which have been circulated to various persons who are at present serving in the Armed Forces.

The statements of Frederick George Frostick, Dennis Oakes, and Edward John Hathway are attached herewith.

The charge of unlawful possession was adjourned 'Sine Die' at Clerkenwell Magistrates Court on the 9th December 1944, when Oldag was committed to take his trial.

I ask authority to hand a copy of this report and statements to the Solicitors Department.
