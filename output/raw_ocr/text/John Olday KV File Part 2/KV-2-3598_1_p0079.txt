10th July, 1945

Dear John,

I expect M.-L. has told you all that has happened to me. I hear that you are comparatively happy, although you will miss a great deal that makes life worth living. And that you have grown a beard? I can't imagine what you will look like. I may be coming to London on holiday soon, & if so, will do my best to visit you.

I am stuck out in the "wilds" & get plenty opportunity for reading & writing. I am studying Marx & Engels' works, & am surprised how far the C.P. deviate from such excellent principles. Marx, in his analysis of social & economic conditions, exposes the system to its depths. I think most of our comrades are too shaky on many points, & would do well to study Marx.

I hope you are able to read & draw. I have a copy of "Kingdom of Rags" now, & often read it & think about you. My pal (whom you met with me once a long time ago, over a year) has been sent to Edinburgh. I miss him but we hope to spend our leaves together, if they coincide, in Glasgow & London (his home).
