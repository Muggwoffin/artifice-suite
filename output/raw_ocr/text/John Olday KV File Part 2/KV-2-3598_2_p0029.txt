Note of Telephone Conversations on 27th and 28th December, 1944

Major Maxwell-Knight, Inspector Whitehead (S.B.) and Mr. Himsworth, rang up and I gathered from them the following story:-

On about 17th November, a man giving the name and carrying the Identity card of Frederick FROSTICK 77, Whitehill Road, Gravesend, was arrested for being in possession of a typewriter believed to be stolen. That matter had been disposed of because the owner of the typewriter, GRIFFIN of Acton, the head of the Hammersmith group of Anarchists, said that it had been lent to FROSTICK.

During the course of these enquiries, however, it had emerged that the man in custody was, in fact, not FROSTICK. He had made a statement that he was an Anarchist who did not believe in law and order, and he refused to give any information about himself. The owner of the Identity card, the real FROSTICK, had been seen and appeared genuine.

The police were satisfied that the false FROSTICK was John OLDAG. He is in custody, awaiting trial, at the Old Bailey, and a police officer who knew him by sight was sent to Brixton on 28.12.44. to have a look at him.

Special Branch had further information that OLDAG had probably been living at Camden Studios, 28b, Camden Street, N.W., also that HEWETSON had a typewriter with interchangeable keys. Special Branch proposed in the near future to apply for a warrant to search Camden Studios and research HEWETSON's premises for the typewriter.

R.G. Micklethwait.
