To: -
Dear Phil,

It is good to know your back again. I can't tell you how I felt when I got those lines you had written in the court. By the way my writing in the letter does not compel you to write if you are too busy as I know you are. The result of my last outburst is a big sized complex. Do you remember the coloured pen drawings, and water colours I did just before my arrest? I now wish we had published them in a surrealist magazine of our own or organised an exhibition, for now after the exhibition of Picasso's paintings nobody is going to believe that Adler and I (inspired by his paintings) created sinister forms and colours without knowing of his works. I have had some very interesting experiences with the people employed here in the Arts and Crafts. Fortunately the staff in charge is quite progressive, and not hostile towards modern art, and being a teacher is experimenting parragocically with the S.J.S. By chint, an utterly uneducated peasant, looking after Cows and Pigs in the most primitive, backward parts of Polands Gale, etc., the lowest parts in the east, the most primitive savage and headhunter has a finer taste for colours, a better colour instinct, and creative imagination than the average men here. I do feel sorry for our A.M.C. staff. It is unbelievable that the working class is corrupted with the "spendo-art" (Kitcher), cultivated by the industrial revolution, and post-war periods. If I could I would utilise my time in producing a number of paintings, drawings, and woodcuts for an exhibition of works produced in detention just in a hopeless attempt to raise the reputation of the modern British Artist, who at present are internationally regarded as fifty years behind progress or mere copists. At any rate my mind is full with sketches for my next publication.

I hear Silverman is arrested. Anything in connection with the F.D.C.?

Don't work too hard.

Yours,

Certified copy and typed by me -

[signature]
No. 10 Military Detention Barrack.
