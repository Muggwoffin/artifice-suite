15 January, 1946

Dear Tallent:

Reference your PF.66074Y/F.3.m relating to Pte A.W. OLDAG.

I have now received from Baker a file on this soldier and also that on Pte A Meltzer.

Both of these soldiers are now at the Military Detention Barrack, Sowerby Bridge Yorkshire, and I have received from the Commandant copies of two letters addressed to Oldag.

The first is dated November 11th, 45 from Oswestry and signed "IVOR". It contains a description of the Armistice Day celebrations and the following extracts indicate the nature of it:-

"How forced and hollow it sounded; No feeling - only glaring heartless nationalistic triumph. The man and - especially - women who gather round the cenotaph are like vultures congregated for a mental feast of bones and withered flesh."

It goes on to say:-

"Yesterday I sent off an article on the Atomic Bomb. I hope it'll be accepted"

The tone of this article seems to have been that as warfare develops it becomes more and more impossible for mere bone and flesh to endure unless prodded at by the eloquence and threats of the Fuhrers.

Finally the correspondence says:-

"I sent a letter and article to the "Daily Mirror" last week. I don't think they were published, but a couple of days ago, the paper had a leader on Glasshouses - demanding an enquiry into Stakehill by civilians and not by any of the "Military Caste". So my letter etc may have had some effect, though I demanded more stringent measures - the total abolition of the system, those "models and fathers of concentration camps"."

The second letter is from 287 Netherton Road, Knightswood, Glasgow W.3 dated 5/1/46 and signed "JESSIE". The only part of it that appears to have any possible interest is as follows:-

"Remember you asked me a long time ago if I had read "The Kingdom of Rags" and I said no, I have done so now and I find you are an entirely different John from the one I know, so remember and introduce me to the author."

This letter also mentions the following names:-
