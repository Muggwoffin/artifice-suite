Rex v John OLDAG @ OLDDAY.

1. The trial of the above named took place this morning at the Central Criminal Court before the Recorder, (Sir Gerald Dodson), and a jury. Mr. R.E. Seaton, (instructed by the Commissioner of Police), appeared on behalf of the prosecution, no Counsel appeared on behalf of OLDAG, though it is thought that Messrs. Smithdale, Rutledge & Co. (Mr. Silverman) were taking an interest in the case.

2. The charges preferred against OLDAG are already fully set out in the Police report, which is to be found in the file, but of these the only one upon which evidence was offered was that in connection with making a false representation that he was John FROSTICK, whose National Registration Identity Card was found in his possession.

3. Throughout the hearing OLDAG offered no defence whatever, but from time to time announced with smug satisfaction that he did not recognise the court at all. On the conclusion of the evidence the Recorder summed up at very short length and directed the jury that there was really no defence to the case whatever.

4. On the jury bringing in a verdict of guilty, the Police officer in charge of the case, Sgt. Bridges, went into the witness box and gave a resume of OLDAG's activities, including the fact that he was a military deserter. In connection with this Sgt. Bridges was somewhat embarrassed by the Recorder's observations and questions, which were directed towards a) how much the Freedom Press paid OLDAG for his cartoons, and b) what was being done about the Freedom Press, which he understood was an Anarchist association. Sgt. Bridges was able to pass these observations over by saying that Freedom Press was the subject of enquiry by Special Branch, and that no information was available as to OLDAG's earnings from them in respect of his cartoons.

5. In passing sentence of twelve month's imprisonment, the Recorder observed that the offence with which OLDAG was charged was a grave one, the maximum penalty being one to two year's imprisonment.
