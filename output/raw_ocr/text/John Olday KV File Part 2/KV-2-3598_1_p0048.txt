Dearest Maria,

Many thanks for your last letter (11/10/45) No need to tell how disappointed I was not to have seen you, G & I. Please do not worry about my being in Stakehill there are worst places in the world and so far I haven't experienced anything worth of any excitement - to a certain extent I am rather pleased to be in this place. Remember when I told Driberg what he ought to do if he really wanted to find the truth? I should have felt hurt if I had been sent to a less notorious camp. In the past I often felt guilty when I thought of my friends who had to suffer so long while I was free. When I thought how Hitten ??died and Erich I felt somehow a cad to be alive. Remember in 'Boston' when the revolutionary was asked by his friend who came to visit him. 'Why on earth are you in prison?' and he answered 'Why are you not!!?' By the way I have collected quite interesting material concerning the death of Forster, how he was executed and what happened to his hangman. Ivor is writing almost daily obvious trying hard to comfort me. Tell him I shall write the next week again. I'm glad to learn that the "I.W." has published to psalms ??. Please do keep all cuttings and copies of interest. I also learn that "D.A." has published in its first issue a cartoon by me. I'm a little embarrassed and upset about it and hope you realise I have not given them any permission. Ask Simon to write to the comrades of the 'I.W.' (the one who writes musings of a Wobbly knows all about me) they might be interested to publish the collection I was talking about. I'm so very glad to know old Ph is now with you and Vero & John will join you soon. Give Pip Walker my fraternal greetings and many thanks for his letter and fags (who is he?).

Tell all comrades not to send any parcels and gifts. Give Lilian a kiss from me. Albert is still in pr. they have taken his clothes away from him while he was bathing. I'm glad he has this experience I know he will make many mental notes which will be of good use later. Tell the Spanish Comrades there is a comrade in Stakehill who sends them his fraternal greetings. (Cervantes could write his Don Quixote in a Spanish Prison, Rosa Luxemburg her Tunies pamphlet in a prison of the Kaiser and Ernst Toller his Schwelben - ballade in a prison of the Weimar Republic. I know a number of Jewish Writers who wrote some essays and poems and plays in the notorious "KZ" of the Nazis. I am positive the Spanish have something to contribute. Please forgive my talking about everything else but you.

I am yours always J O.

Your last two letters were the sweetest I ever had.

Address: Mrs. M.L. Richards.
Elton Place
Haverstock Hill.
London. NW.3.

Certified that this copy of letter written by A.W. Oldag was typed by me.

Stakehill.
23rd Oct. 45.

Major.
Asst. Commandant.
