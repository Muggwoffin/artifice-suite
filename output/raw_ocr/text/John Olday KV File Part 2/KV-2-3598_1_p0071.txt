My dear Simon,

Many thanks for the pleasant news. I can't tell you how glad it makes me to know that—at last—"Free-unions" are in print. I feel sure this new publication is going to be a hit. The friends of "Now" are bound to support it. In addition it will conquer the imagination of the young. I am proud to be one of its contributors. It pleases me also very much to learn you like the other poems, in spite of their non-surrealist character. If some of them find only half as much resonance in the hearts of the workers as the poems of Erich found, I shall be more than richly rewarded. I do not regard the English written poems as good poetry. They are the outcome of an impulse which forces me to operate playfully with the few hundred English words I have captured. I am still thinking & dreaming in German. Don't you agree that, for example the "Elenos Hyume" is a good poem? The English written are mere experiments and deserve only to be recognized as such. (But still, I like to hear you say they are excellent.)—I do hope you have given old Phil also a break. He does deserve it!
