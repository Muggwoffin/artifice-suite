On this latter date he was further charged with the offences with which he now stands indicted. Evidence was then given by Frederick Frostick of 77, Whitehill Road, Gravesend, Kent, that the Identity Card shewn to him by Police and bearing his name and address was his property. He explained that on or about 20th May 1944, he had lost the Identity Card in question, in the Gravesend district. No person other than himself was authorised to use the Card and concluded by saying that the accused was unknown to him.

The accused was further remanded in custody until the 9th December 1944, so that Police could make further enquiries in an effort to establish the prisoner's identity.

On the remand hearing on 9th December 1944, the accused as on previous hearings adopted a mute attitude and took no interest in the proceedings. As at this time it could not be ascertained his real identity, he was committed to take his trial at the Central Criminal Court as a man (name refused).

In view of the nature of this case Special Branch was contacted, with a result that enquiries made by Officers of that Branch, proved that the prisoner's correct name was Arthur William Oldag.

Identification was brought about by Detective Sergeant Edward John Hathway of Special Branch, when visiting Brixton Prison on the 2nd January 1945, for that purpose. The prisoner when questioned on this point, then admitted his identity. Additional evidence to this effect will be given at the trial by the Officer concerned. The accused has been duly informed of this fact and also served with a copy of the evidence to be given.

There are no previous convictions recorded against Oldag in this country.

Arthur William Oldag @ Olday, claims to be a British subject, having been born in London on 10th April, 1905, the illegitimate son of a German mother and a Scottish father. He states that he was taken, as a baby to New York, and at the age of eight to Germany, where he was brought up. During the last years of his stay in Germany he was employed as a journalist and cartoonist, but claims to have been engaged in anti-Nazi activities there, as a result of which, he left that country in 1937.

On arrival in England in 1937, Oldag found lodgings at 54, Lillington Street, Victoria, London. He later lived with Rose Fyleman, an American authoress, at 10, Luxborough House, Luxborough Street, London, S.W.1. This woman translated Oldag's book 'Kingdom of Rags', which sold 500 copies and was published by Messrs Jarrolds under the pseudonym 'John Olday'.

After quarrelling with Fyleman, he went to live at 57, Greek Street, Soho. In September 1938, he married Hilda Meisel, a Hungarian born in Vienna. It is believed that they parted company shortly after the marriage and there are grounds for suspecting that it was one of convenience in order that the wife could obtain British Nationality.
