Pte. A.W. Oldag.

COPY.

Oswestry.
24th Feb. 1946.

My Dear John,

I am sorry my last letter ended so suddenly. But I wanted to ensure that you would get it Saturday, and I had forgotten that the mails here are collected early in the afternoons. You will be happy to know I am able to get up and walk about a little now. My caliper is not very comfortable but, like other minor tortures of life, I shall get used to it. In case the word "caliper" means nothing to you, it looks like this - (Sketch) - The sketch isn't very convincing but you can see that the ring is meant to be fitted round the thigh, while the prongs at the other end, fit into holes made in the heel of my shoe. I'm glad to be walking again anyway.

Great struggles everywhere: mutinies and riots in India, put down by brute force - the casualties go into four figures, probably several thousands by now. In Egypt a revolution is being strangled by British troops. In Spain, left-wingers have been shot, and arms and sabateurs are still secretly crossing the Pyrenees from France, where popular indignation against the executions runs high. In Palestine the Jews are still hitting out. An interesting thing is that the Cairo Jews and Egyptians have sunk their antagonisms and united against the Government and its possible imperial ally. In Sicily and indeed all Southern Italy, there seem to be small spasmodic insurrections. Oh! a most amusing thing - You remember a week or two ago the R.A.F. were striking in India. Well, they are now being blamed for having precipitated the present avalanche of mutiny and rebellion, by setting "a bad example."

To come to England, there has been a riot in Aldershot D.B., windows and sky-lights smashed and the rioters climbing on the roofs. The Government are planning to make tests of atomic bombs. Why are the public so sterile and inactive? Are they going to see these devilish creations piling up for future use - and not do something about it? Nearly everyday we read in the newspapers about some improvements to the mechanism of war - a new bombing or fighter plane being produced, etc. In the American Senate recently, somebody was lamenting that USA had only half as many aircraft as Britain.

I wish I knew when I would be released from the Army. I do not even know my demob. number, because I don't understand how time spent absent and in detention reckons against me - by how much my time or number is increased.

I wrote twice to my former unit but, receiving no reply, I sent a letter to my records office. That was 3 weeks ago and I have not yet had answer.

I was speaking to a chap here yesterday who had been to S'by Bridge about a year ago. He told me what sort of place it was. He had only been in for a fortnight, but remembered it very vividly. It was exactly one year ago to-day that I went to Chorley. Here's a trifle I wrote on one of the bright mornings of last week, which may amuse you.

You pale hospital patients!
Don't you find your spirits drawn out of you and away into the sunlight
This fine sharp morning of spring?
Aren't your nerveless feet fretting,
Your sunken muscles disturbed from their sleep in the dark world of
blankets - Agitating for freedom on the sunlit upland slopes?
The Sun looks inquisitively through the open windows:
What are you doing, you children of Earth, Lying there, hiding
yourselves?
Sick, you say? Injured, wounded? invalid?
I don't know what those words mean, but I can see all is not as
it should be.
Look! I touch your cheeks in pity.
If only you could come under my care!
I have more cunning medicine here than in those bottles blushing
under my glance.

I can give a transfusion from my veins that will make your blood
sing in your bodies.

Listen! My comrade air calls you too.
He is dancing on the field this morning, Strong in his gladness.
Stripping the far hills of their mysteries
Like an atheist whose laughter dissolves religions.
Come out with us! We stretch our wide arms out.
To draw you into our company.
Why are you hanging back?
But the pale hospital patients

P.T.O.
