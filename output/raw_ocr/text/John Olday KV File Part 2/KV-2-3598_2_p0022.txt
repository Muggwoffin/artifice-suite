SPECIAL SEARCH
METROPOLITAN POLICE
34 JAN 1945

Somers Town Station,
'N' Division.

3rd January, 1945

To Divisional Detective Inspector

Arthur William Oldag, age 39, a journalist, of no fixed abode, stands committed to take his trial at the Central Criminal Court commencing 9th January 1945 on the following indictment:-

1. 'Did feloniously, on or about the 20th day of May 1944, at Gravesend, Kent, steal a National Registration Identity Card No. DIW/659, in the name of Frederick Frostick, value 1s. the property of the said Frederick Frostick.'
Contrary to Section 2 of the Larceny Act, 1916.

2. 'Did feloniously, on or about the 20th day of May, 1944, at Gravesend, Kent, receive the above mentioned Identity Card, knowing it to have been stolen.'
Contrary to Section 33 of the said Statute.

3. 'Did unlawfully, on the 17th day of November 1944, at Eversholt Street, N.W.1. use a name, to wit, Frederick Frostick, other than that by which he was ordinarily known prior to the coming into force of the Defence (General) Regulations, 1939.'
Contrary to Regulation 20, of the said Regulations.

4. 'Did unlawfully, on the 17th day of November 1944, at Eversholt Street, N.W.1. with intent to deceive, falsely represent that he was the person to whom an Identity Card related.
Contrary to Section 8 (1) (b) (i) of the National Registration Act, 1939.

The facts of the case are as follow:-

At about 1.45 p.m. on the 17th November 1944, Police Sergeant Dennis Oakes, 46 'N' Division, was patrolling with other officers in a Police Car at Eversholt Street, N.W.1. when they saw Oldag walking along that thoroughfare. The Police Sergeant stopped him told him who he was, and requested that he produce his Identity Card for examination.

The Identity Card was produced which shewed that it was allotted to a Frederick Frostick of 77, Whitehill Road, Gravesend, Kent, No. DIW/659:759. Oldag on the production of this Card stated that the name and address thereon related to himself. After further questioning by the Police Sergeant on this point he decided to detain Oldag so that further enquiries could be made. He was taken to Somers Town Police Station where again being challenged as to his correct name and address Oldag replied, "I refuse to acknowledge the laws of this country and I am not going to say any more.

He was later charged that day with being in the unlawful possession of a hyperdermic syringe and a 'Parker' fountain pen. The property the subject of this charge was found in Oldag's possession upon arrest.
