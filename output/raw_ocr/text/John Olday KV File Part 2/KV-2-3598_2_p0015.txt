At the trial OLDAG was undefended and he pleaded "Not Guilty" to all the charges. The police prosecution was conducted by Mr. Reginald E. SEATON, Barrister-at-Law, 1, Garden Court, Temple, E.C.4.

Details of the case for the prosecution were given by Mr. Seaton and evidence of arrest on 17th November, 1944, and of the making of a false statement, was given by Sergeant. Dennis Oakes, 46.N. Division. Frederick Frostick, of 77, Whitehill Road, Gravesend, Kent, was called and proved that the Identity Card produced by OLDAG on his arrest was the witness' property, which he had lost on or about 20th May, 1944.

I gave evidence to the effect that the prisoner was identical with a man, whom I had interviewed at 54, Lillington Street, S.W.1. on 22nd July, 1940 and who then had admitted that his correct name was Arthur William OLDAG and had produced documents of identity in that name.

The prisoner asked no questions of any of the witnesses.

At this juncture, after some discussion between the Judge and Counsel for the prosecution, it was decided to drop the first three charges and proceed on the last.

Asked by the Judge if he wished to say anything in his defence, OLDAG, who had maintained an air of indifference throughout, made the following remarks from the dock, "I am an anarchist and as such I do not recognise the authority of the Court or justice and that is why I pleaded 'Not Guilty'." The Judge remarked, "You had better say that again. I do not understand." and OLDAG replied, "I do not recognise the Court and it is immaterial to me what you decide."

After a brief summing up of the case by the Judge the Jury returned a verdict of "Guilty" without retiring.

The prisoner's antecedents were then given by Detective Constable W.J. Bridges, Somers Town, "N" Division.
