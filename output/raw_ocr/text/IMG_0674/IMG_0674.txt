Fineberg rumor (and I must confess here to a fact that he is a damn good sort, and he has got a real good brain & I really think we were all unfair about him. I never really knew how nice he was in London). Anyhow I come back to my tale of woe. Fineberg had translated the theses and had understood all the speeches (as he knows about a dozen law progs) and he was consequently much better informed on the subject than I was. We therefore agreed that he would be the best person to do the job. And then this of what happened. We were both very busy immediately after the conference. Sort of after work, then he per- fectly, and then I had to go away. Since your letter referring to the subject we have again discussed it, and he has agreed to do it 'say for the Poultry'. But he has to get hold of the three first, it'll way take a day or two. And at the very end of time, I warn him Moscow, which I will repeat to the end of time.
