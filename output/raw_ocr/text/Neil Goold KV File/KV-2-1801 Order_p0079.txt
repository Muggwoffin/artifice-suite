Extract from letter from MOOS mentioning GOOLD-VERSCHOYLE

When Brian worked at the E.E.C. in Stafford, he began to learn Russian. His brother, Neil, who was in every respect his ideal, lived and was married in Moscow. His wife, Olga Ivanowna, usually called Loila, wrote many very charming letters to Brian. Brian belonged to the Communist Youth (or Party?) and felt somehow that Russia and Neil's family was his home. He went to Russia in 1933 (July). Loila received him magnificently, she introduced him to many nice girls and Irina ADLER who had been an acquaintance of Neil, asked him to stay in her flat. She was married with an engineer. After 8 days, Brian felt that he wanted to stay in Moscow his contract with the E.E.C. was almost terminated. Loila encouraged him and helped him to find a job as an electrician in a Moscow factory. However at the last moment when the consent of the police was needed, Loila introduced him to 2 people (these were probably her relatives as Mr. KRIVITSKY told me in New York) who wore a dark green uniform and made a great secret of their meeting. These 2 people said to B. that the permission could not have been given yet, that he ought to return home as a good Communist, but that he ought to offer or give his services to the Soviet Union in every capacity which he was not told, and that then he would be allowed to come back to Russia and live there happily ever after. Brian then was just 21, he signed some form of contract (I think it was an oath) and was sent home. He expected to hear from the people.
