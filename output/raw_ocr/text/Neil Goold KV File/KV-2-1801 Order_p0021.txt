POLITICAL PRISONERS.
Charles Smith also addressed the meeting, and referred to the police force as the "Brox Harriers," and to the Army as the "Free State Army." He challenged these forces to fight. He referred to the treatment of political prisoners, and said they were not allowed to hear Mass or have Rosary beads, and that they were confined for twenty-two hours a day. Referring to the murder of Dan-Sergi Denis O'Brien, Smith said that O'Brien had been "that he would shoot all Republican driers, and that band of G. had fallen on O'Brien."
Referring to the shooting named Jackie Griffith in the case of Dublin Smith said that the police had taken the prisoner, and that the prisoner had been taken to the police station, and refused to allow friends to go to his aid.
Joseph O'Connor also spoke at the meeting, and said that when all Republicans were united in the Republican Unity Committee they would arm themselves and fight "to the last" the Free State forces. The fight would be a bitter one, and blood would be spilled. Other detective officers gave evidence of the speeches made.
NO DISPLAYS OF ENTHUSIASM.
Detective Garda T. J. Morris, replying to a member of the Court, said that there were about 40 or 50 people at the meeting, and there were no displays of enthusiasm.
Cross-examined by Goold, he denied that he struck Goold in the face with his fan in the Bridewell, but he admitted that he had used force to take Goold's fingerprints.
Detective Garda T. Boyle gave evidence of arresting Goold, and of finding certain documents in a locker belonging to Goold in the Iveagh Buildings. One document headed "Resolutions of Principles" contained the following heads:
1. That it is our first task as part of the International Revolutionary working-class movement to do all in our power to preserve, defend and strengthen the Soviet State as the bulwark of the world proletariat.
2. That in view of the world revolutionary events now taking place we should confirm and put forward with increased vigour the demand for the Irish Republic.
3. We mean by the Irish Republic the Irish people, united and in arms and organised as the rulers of their own country.
4. That the Irish Free State can never grow into the Irish Republic, but that, on the contrary, the Irish Republic can only come into existence in a relentless struggle against the Free State and Stormont State.
"MINIMUM PROGRAMME."
5. That the principal demands of the "Minimum Programme," i.e., nationalisation of the monopolies under the Republic, are inseparable from the demand for an Irish Republic.
6. That we should demand an Irish Republic with a seat on the World Security Organisation, since the Irish Free State could not speak for the Irish people in such an organisation.
7. But before this can be a real demand the Irish people must be armed and united and a force in their own land.
8. They can only become this as a result of a struggle against the monopoly capitalist State which is keeping them in subjection.
9. All economic struggles must be subordinated to this main political struggle.
10. Since the reformist labour parties believe in collaboration with the State, and believe that the State will grow into a democratic Republic as a result of a Labour Government being voted into power, the main field of such a struggle cannot be the Labour Party.
11. The main struggle must, therefore, be outside the Labour Party with the forces, both Communist and non-Socialist Republican, which have never accepted the Free State.
12. Thus, a united or single Republic Front must be created.
13. The lines along which such a Republican Front could be formed was shown in the actual strength which took place in protest at Charlie Kerins' execution. The break-up of these forces was due to the retreat of some of the Communists.
14. It is now time to take up this struggle again, the main attack being against all the measures restricting civil liberties, since only the restoration of such liberties can give the democratic forces an opportunity to organise.
This document was dated May 5, 1945.
Det.-Garda Boyle said that the document contained minutes of the Committee's meetings, of group members' attitude in Court, their attitude to the Presidential, Dail, Senate and municipal elections, and details of the Committee's Constitution.
