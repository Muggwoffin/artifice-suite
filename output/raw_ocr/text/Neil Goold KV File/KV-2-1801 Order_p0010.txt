DUBLINER WAS "SPIRITED" TO RUSSIA.

It has just been revealed in a lawsuit in Paris that a young Irishman who was spirited out of Spain in 1937 by Russian agents, was taken to the Soviet Union and there sentenced to a long term of penal servitude.

He is Brian Goold-Verschoyle, youngest son of the late Mr. and Mrs. Goold-Verschoyle. Although the term of imprisonment - ten years - has obviously expired nothing has been heard of the Dubliner since 1937.

A radio engineer by profession, Brian had visited Russia early in the Nineteen-thirties. When the Spanish Civil War broke out in 1936, he went to Spain as a radio operator for the Government. He was then aged 24.

In the spring of 1937 his letters home suddenly ceased. Inquiries by his parents and by British Consular officials in Spain brought little tangible information though the young man was traced to Barcelona. But there the trail ended.

In 1939 there appeared a book, "I was Stalin's Agent," by a man named Krevitsky, who revealed how Brian - whom he called Pat - was enticed on to a Russian boat at Barcelona and taken to Russia. Krevitsky also privately informed the parents that he had last heard of their son in a Moscow prison late in 1937. The parents have since died believing their son to be dead.

Several near relatives of Brian Goold-Verschoyle are still alive; some are believed to reside in Ireland. His parents originally came from Donegal.

The law suit in Paris - which to-day entered its last week - is that in which ex-Soviet official Victor Kravchenko, author of "I Choose Freedom," is suing for libel the Pro-Communist weekly, "Les Lettres Francaises."

("Evening Herald" dated 14th March, 1949.)
