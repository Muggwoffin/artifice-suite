P.F.414.26/B.I.H.

5th October 1942.

Dear Moore,

In accordance with our recent telephone conversation I send you the following particulars of Hamilton Neil Stuart GOOLD-VERSCHOYLE.

He was born in Dublin on 19.12.04 (almost in the purple!) and is one of a family which lives at The Manor House, Dunkineely, County Donegal.

On 1.10.30, he went to Russia and worked in Moscow as a translator, and is believed to have married a Russian wife, and been engaged as a state servant in education and broadcasting. He was expelled from Russia for some reason, but on his return to Eire, threw himself into work for the Communist Party of Ireland, and adopted the more plebeian name of Neil GOOLD. The adoption of this alias led Frank AIKEN to attack him "for being here under several aliases" when he was playing a leading part in demonstrations by the unemployed and tenants movement. (This comes from an article in the CONNOLLY Club paper, IRISH FREEDOM, of January 1941, which says that "Neil" has been thrown into prison many times, and once the police authorities tried to have him certified as insane to stop his activities.) GOOLD challenged AIKEN in the Irish Workers' Weekly, but the Communist Party of Ireland seems to have supported him and a Bulletin which he was issuing on behalf of a tenants Committee in Dublin. Jim PRENDERGAST, one of the leaders of the Communist Party in Dublin, wrote a letter on 25.2.41, in which he said that "our old friend GOOLD opposes our main line and in all his work is carrying a line opposite to that of the Party."

GOOLD is reported to have been arrested with other Communists in Dublin on 3.5.41, for attempting to distribute pamphlets advocating non-payment of rent by certain tenants of the Dublin Corporation. He was believed to be still interned, though the last we heard of him was a letter which he wrote from Tintown Internment Camp, at the Curragh, on 6.8.41, to "Olga", Ivanovna, Dobrovna, at Poste Restante, Moscow.
