Another document containing minutes of the Committee's meetings discussed the I.R.A. and the attitude the Committee was to adopt towards that organisation.

Apparently the Committee considers that the I.R.A. was too much of a mixture of both capitalists and proletarians. "Our attitude, therefore, should be in our work within the I.R.A. to win over the majority to a truly revolutionary policy and methods of struggle, by fighting the reactionary elements in a democratic manner, and this not by opposing the I.R.A. as a form of organisation but by preserving its truly revolutionary character and never voluntarily retiring from it. Our attitude to the I.R.A. outside its ranks should be to support it in so far as it conducts a struggle genuinely directed against the State of a kind that in the last resort can, and will rally the support of the masses of the Irish people who are oppressed by the State."

The Committee of Republican Unity does not recognise the State Dail as legally constituted and it envisages the coming revival of the Republican movement taking place in three stages:-

1. A period of peaceful propaganda to win back the Irish people to the principles of the Irish Republic.
2. A period of organisation to unite all anti-Imperialists, that is the vast masses of the people, in a democratic Republican front.
3. A period of struggle, during which the people will assert their demands against the Imperialist forces that are keeping them in subjection, from which struggle they must emerge armed, organised and united as masters of their own destiny.

To me this was a very interesting case. Before the details were made public it seemed as if Goold and his colleagues were just part of the I.R.A. organisation, as, indeed, they may be. But the documents disclose quite another aspect. Their whole tone savours greatly of our old friends, the Irish Republican Brotherhood.
