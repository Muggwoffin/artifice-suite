SECRET.

NIALL GOOLD.

Real name Hamilton Niall Steward Gould-Verschoyle.

Early history uncertain but believed born in Dublin of well-to-do parents. Educated at Oxford. He is stated to have married a Russian and to have two children living in Russia.

Goold became prominently identified with Communist activities in Dublin during the early war years and was arrested and interned at the Curragh Internment Camp in 1941. He, apparently, made good use of his incarceration to further the Communist cause amongst the I.R.A. political prisoners with whom he shared imprisonment. He organised political educational classes and taught the Russian language in the Camp. By the time he was transferred to Mountjoy Prison at the end of 1942 he had organised a Communist group in the Camp. He endeavoured to continue his efforts in Mountjoy Prison but was released in December, 1942, before he could make much progress.

After release, he kept in touch with his principal followers by letter and saw to the regular flow of Communist literature to both the Prison and Internment Camp. Later, as his followers were released, he contacted them and gradually drew them together again. His objective was the formation of a militant section of the Communist Party but his efforts were largely unsuccessful.

In 1945, Goold broke with the Communist Party of Ireland and tried, without success, to establish his small following as the legitimate upholders of Communism and Irish Nationalism.

He formed a "Committee of Republican Unity," the first-declared task of which was to do all in its power "to preserve, defend and strengthen the Soviet State as the bulwark of the world proletariat," and to put forward with increased vigour the demand for the Irish Republic.

As a result of his efforts in this connection, Goold appeared before the Special Criminal Court, Dublin, in January, 1946, charged with advocating and encouraging the formation of an armed force not authorised by or under the Constitution. He was found guilty and received a sentence of twelve months' imprisonment, to terminate after six months if he entered into bail to keep the peace for two years.
