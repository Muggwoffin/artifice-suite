ADVOCATED ARMED FORCE
THREE MEN SENTENCED
THREE men appeared before the Special Criminal Court in Dublin, yesterday, charged with membership of an unlawful organisation and with advocating and encouraging the usurpation of the functions of government, namely, by advocating and encouraging the formation and maintenance of an armed force not authorised by or under the Constitution.

The men were: Niall Goold, Iveagh Buildings, Bride Street, Dublin; Joseph O'Connor, 44D Lower Dorset Street, Dublin, and Charles Smith, Mount Tallant Avenue, Harold's Cross.

Mr. A. O'Keeffe (instructed by Mr. Liam Lysaght, Chief State Solicitor's Office), who prosecuted, said that he proposed to enter a nolle prosequi against a fourth man who had been charged, Liam O'Reilly, Devenish Road, Crumlin.

When Goold and O'Connor were asked to plead to the charges, they said: "We demand trial by jury." Smith said: "I would like the Court to prove that I am a member of an illegal organisation."

Pleas of not guilty were entered by direction of the Court. Goold and O'Connor, who were found guilty, were each sentenced to twelve months' imprisonment from the date of arrest, to terminate after six months if they enter bail of £50 to keep the peace for two years. Smith, who was also found guilty, was sentenced to three months, not to take effect if he enters £50 bail to keep the peace for two years.

Mr. O'Keeffe said that the charges arose out of a public meeting held at Cathal Brugha St. on the night of Sept. 9 last, under the auspices of the Republican Unity Committee. The speakers included the three accused men.

Sergt. John Mulvey said that on September 9 he was present at the meeting in Cathal Brugha Streets, which was being addressed by Goold.

OBJECTS OF COMMITTEE.
Goold said that the objects of the Republican Unity Committee were the unity of all Republican forces, organisation and revolution. He said that there would have to be a revolution to overthrow State control, even though that revolution would mean the spilling of blood.

He dealt with passages in the doctrines of Thomas Davis, who, he said, had opposed revolution. He referred to the Davis centenary celebrations which, he said, had been organised by Dumas, O'Donnell, Michael Loughney. He said that the expulsion of Cathal Kerins and others by the Government was illegal, because the Government itself was illegally constituted.

POLITICAL PRISONERS.
Charles Smith also addressed the meeting and referred to the police force as "the Iron Harriers" "to the Army as the Free State Army." He challenged these forces to fight. He referred to the treatment of political prisoners and said they were not allowed to hear Mass or have Rosary beads, and that they were confined for twenty-two hours in the day. Referring to the murder of Det-Sergt. Denis O'Brien, Smith said that O'Brien had boasted "that he would shoot all Republican prisoners. The last hand of O'Brien had fallen on O'Brien."

Referring to the meeting, he named Jackie Griffin in the Dublin Smith said that the police had surrounded the streets, and refused to allow civilians to go to his aid.

Joseph O'Connor also spoke at the meeting, and said that when all Republicans were united in the Republic, all Irishmen would arm themselves and fight "against the Free State forces. The fight would be a bitter one, and blood would be spilt." Other detective officers gave evidence of the speeches made.

NO DISPLAYS OF ENTHUSIASM
Detective Garda T. J. Morra, replying to a member of the Court, said that there were about 40 or 50 people at the meeting and there were no displays of enthusiasm.

Cross-examined by Goold, he denied that he struck Goold in the face with his fist in the Bridewell, but he admitted that he had used force to take Goold's fingerprints.

ATTITUDE TO I.R.A.
"Being an organisation of a class which is constantly growing with both proletarians on the one hand, and capitalists on the other, the I.R.A. naturally gives birth to genuine revolutionaries on the one hand, and persons of fascist leanings on the other.

"Our attitude, therefore, should be in our work within the I.R.A. to win over the majority to a truly revolutionary policy and methods of struggle, by fighting the reactionary elements in a democratic manner, and this not by opposing the I.R.A. as a form of organisation but by presenting its truly revolutionary character and never voluntarily retiring from it.

"Our attitude to the I.R.A. from outside its ranks should be to support it in so far as it conducts a struggle, genuinely directed against the State, of a kind that in the last resort can, and will, rally the support of the masses of the Irish people who are oppressed by the State."

In reference to the Dail elections, the document said: "We do not recognise the State Dail as legally constituted. Therefore, we support candidates only for the purpose of using the Dail as a platform to rally the people to the struggle for the demands of the working class, and for the establishment of the Republic by the dissolution of the present State.

PLAN OF CAMPAIGN.
After discussion, stated the document, it was agreed that the following should be embodied in the Committee's programme as their plan of campaign:
1. That this Committee envisages the coming revival of the Republican movement taking place in three stages:
1. A period of peaceful propaganda during which the Irish people must be won back to the principles of the Irish Republic from which, during the last years, they retreated to the pressure from the armed forces of the State.
2. A period of organisation during which all anti-imperialist forces, that is, the vast masses of the Irish people, must become united in a democratic Republican front.
3. A period of struggle, during which the Irish people will assert their demands against the Imperialist forces that are keeping them in subjection, from which struggle they must emerge armed, organised and united as the masters of their destiny.
