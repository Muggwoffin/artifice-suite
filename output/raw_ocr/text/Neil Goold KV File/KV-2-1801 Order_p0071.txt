Eileen was the last member of the family whom I saw. She and her husband were on leave and I saw them three or four times in Donegal in the summer of 1938. She talked freely to me of Neil and Brian and was obviously extremely worried at the lack of news from Brian in Spain. Neil, she told me, had gone to Russia, where he had married and been engaged as a state servant in education and broadcasting. He had been expelled from Russia and according to her story, (which I believe she received direct from Neil,) he had been unable to take his wife or child with him. He was at that time helping the Communist party in Dublin by performing extremely unresponsible jobs, such as selling the Daily Worker. She told me, that, so far from being disillusioned by his treatment at the hands of the Communist party he considered their action entirely correct. In his view as Russia had been so permeated with foreign agents the Communist party was right to expel from the country all those of whom they had any doubt at all. As they had entertained doubts about his bona fides they rightly made no compassionate exception on his behalf. But he hoped by his work for the party in Ireland to convince them that he was in every way an ardent and bona fide Communist and fit to be allowed to return.
