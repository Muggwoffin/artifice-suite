As regards NEUBEGINNEN, I cannot share Mrs. Lambert's view that most of its members are harmless. Practically all contacts I have consulted on this point agree that many of the members are ex-communists who have quarrelled with Stalin's policy rather than with Communism. They remain communists at heart and may be in touch with anti-Stalin communist organisation founded by the late Leon Trotsky and others. It also contains a number of Austrian Revolutionary Socialist, who although officially anti-communist are definitely in favour of securing rule of the proletariat by revolutionary methods and who are generally regarded as almost as dangerous as Communists. Then, according to Hans Jaeger we find in the group "those who have a weak sentimental attachment to Russia or are against the war for other (doctrinaire) reasons." There are some decent elements in the group, such as Schoettle, but on the whole it is regarded with some suspicion by persons who know something about it.
On the other hand, I agree with Mrs. Lambert and disagree with Captain Johnson's statement that the code "contains a surprising number of references to Communist organisations, personalities and persons." It is only natural that there should be code words for the Comintern and the German Communist Party, because reference must be continually made to these by opponents of communism. These two code words are counterbalanced by many others for orthodox legitimate left parties, and it is interesting to note that there is a word "Dan" for the leader of the Russian Menshevik (wrongly spelt) Party in the Allgemeine Korrespondenz of Ernst Loewenheim. This in itself would imply opposition to communism or the Stalin brand but one wonders whether the need for a code word for Trotsky implies relations with anti-Stalinist communism. If so, it would bear out the opinions of my contacts that these communist offshoots found a working field in Neubeginnen.
We may also note a code word for Otto Bauer, the Austrian Revolutionary Socialist who was responsible for the attempted revolution in Vienna in 1934. Relations with him do not imply restriction of the Loewenheim activities to peaceful democratic methods.
There is also a code word for Willi Muenzenberg. This man who was one of the first German communists and resided for a long time in Moscow eventually quarrelled with the Communist Party, but probably more on grounds of opposition to Stalin than to Communism. His revolutionary tendencies remained unchanged, and the need for a code word may probably imply close relations with this dangerous person, who was recently murdered by either the Gestapo or the G.P.U.
I cannot, however, find in the code the name of any prominent communist of the Stalinist variety. It may be that some of the code words for which no explanation is given refer to less prominent communists unknown to me, and I think it would be advisable for the Loewenheims to be asked to identify all persons named in the code but at present undesignated, especially those for whom Christian names are obvious incognitos.
