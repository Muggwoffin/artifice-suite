He said that he came to ask her whether she could do anything about getting him a loan of half the sums amounting to £40 which he had made as a donation to the Society of Friends. He mixed up his queries about the LOEWENHEIMS with this business, which is a genuine one. In this way he elucidated the following information from Mrs. [illegible].

She feels certain that the LOEWENHEIMS were supplied with facts and figures which must have originated from a Government Officer prior to their Internment. In April 1940 the LOEWENHEIMS told her about things which were happening with reference to the German invasion of Denmark and Norway, and the German campaign in France. She was later surprised to find this information made public, so that the LOEWENHEIMS appeared to have got it at a time when it was still considered "inside".

In April, 1941, the LOEWENHEIMS also told her that there would be a general Internment in May, which again proved to be correct. At that time they appeared to be very much afraid, although she could not say of what.

With regard to sources of information, [illegible] wondered whether they might come through Traute, the divorced wife, who has married a man who was a former Secretary of Lord CECIL'S and who is also very friendly with Ellen WILKINSON. Mrs. [illegible] says, from her knowledge of the LOEWENHEIMS, she does not believe that they have ever ceased to be members of the Communist Party. She thinks that they continued to transmit information all the time up to their Internment. We may also note that Mrs. Walter LOEWENHEIM applied for papers as a Soviet citizen at the Russian Embassy after the outbreak of war, which she would be unlikely to do if her husband had left the Party.
