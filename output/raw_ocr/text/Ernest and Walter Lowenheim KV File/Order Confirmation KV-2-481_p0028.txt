Source: Kaspar.  13th October 1944.

Walter and Ernst LOEWENHEIM.

The brothers LOEWENHEIM are Jews who formerly dealt in timber in Berlin. They enjoyed a university technical education. Both belonged to the Central Union of Employees. They joined the Socialist Labour Youth, but went over to the German section of the Young Communist League. In 1927 or 1928 they returned to the Social Democratic Party, in which they cut no figures, but simply subsided into the mass of anonymous party members.

It was not until the end of 1931 or the beginning of 1932 that they emerged as the leaders of a small group of intellectuals who regarded themselves as the left wing of the Social Democratic Party and criticised its 'rightists' from a theoretical Marxian standpoint. This group was numerically and influentially so insignificant that the Party Committee had no knowledge of its existence, which first came to their notice when they had emigrated to Prague in 1933.

In contrast to its insignificance, however, this group engaged from its inception in increasing conspirative activities and employed methods modelled on those used by the Communist Apparat. Even before the Nazis came to power their followers used various cover names; they also employed persons to spy upon internal events in leading Social Democratic circles, etc. In this way some of them, as for instance Heinrich JAKUBOWITZ, whose father was on friendly terms with Dr. Paul HERTZ, succeeded in establishing some control of the Bureau of the Reichstag Group.

Shortly before the underground period Dr. FRANK-MUELLER joined the group, which derived its name and programme from a pamphlet published under the title of Neubeginnen in 1933 by Walter LOEWENHEIM, who wrote it under the pseudonym of 'Miles'. Its principles were in accordance with Otto BAUER's. Then FRANK-MUELLER was appointed to manage the group's so-called 'Foreign Bureau' in Prague, because Miles thought wrongly that he would hold a more important position if he remained in Berlin. But FRANK-MUELLER was cunning enough to employ the conspirative tactics evolved by Miles against the latter. He employed his own liaison men, both in Germany and abroad, to such good purpose that eventually he wrested the whole organization out of Miles' hands and into his own. When Miles hastened indignantly to Prague stormy scenes took place in the presence of Otto BAUER, in the course of which Miles called FRANK-MUELLER a 'rotter, thief and scoundrel'.
