3. Mrs. (who is interned in Holloway Gaol) knows both the brothers LOEWENHEIM and at a recent interview with B.10m she described Walter LOEWENHEIM as a "red hot Communist." When asked "What do you mean by a red hot Communist? Do you mean that he parades about in demonstrations and waves a red flag?" She replied quite spontaneously, "No, I do not mean that. Walter LOEWENHEIM is a very clever man and if he is active in Communist matters, he is active in the background."

Mrs. name should not be mentioned to the Hurst Committee, but she may be referred to as "a source whom we believe to be reliable."

4. In 1929 it was reported that the LOEWENHEIMs' firm in Berlin had amalgamated with another German firm to form the Metropolitan Trading Company, which was understood to handle Soviet funds in Shanghai. This suspicion was partially confirmed.

5. After their arrival in England, the LOEWENHEIMs formed a Company known as the THAMES GENERAL INVESTMENT COMPANY, Ltd., which carried on business under the name of the THAMES ENGINEERING COMPANY.

There is no evidence that this Company has been used for espionage purposes, but the background of the case bears a striking similarity to other cases in which Soviet espionage has been established.

6. It is true that the evidence put before Sir Cecil Hurst sh anti-Nazi activities on the part of the LOEWENHEIMs but this is by no means inconsistent with the evidence of their having been engaged in espionage on behalf of some other foreign power.

7. In view of the foregoing there can be no doubt, from the security point of view, that the application by the LOEWENHEIMs for release from internment should be resisted.
