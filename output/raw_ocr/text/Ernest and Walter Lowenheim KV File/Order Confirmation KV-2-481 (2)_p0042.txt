It will be seen that when the matter of underground work is introduced the Lowenheims' statements fail to coincide in many points. Both became secretive and evasive. When drawn into admitting a code they deny direct knowledge or possession thereof. When names were discussed they became suspicious. They hasten to deny political activity here or the use of code except for communication with Prague. When faced with the code their agitation and shock were very obvious.

When asked to explain names they both suffered from loss of memory, Walter being the worst in this respect, and both asked to consult each other to refresh their memories.

The loss of memory was partially explained by Walter who expressed his fear that if the aliases of people imprisoned in Germany became known there it might be fatal for them.

The loss of memory is more suspicious from the fact that letters to and from their wives and friends refer to people who appear to be mentioned in the code but about whom the Lowenheims now plead ignorance.

REACTIONS.

I gained the impression that the Lowenheims are more concerned with founding and leading subversive political movements than following political ideals. When they lose leadership they drop the group and start another on a different foundation. With their Communist beginnings they are quite likely to swing in that direction and become a menace.

It may be noted that though the code is said to relate to a Social Democrat Movement and probably does so, it contains a somewhat surprising number of references to Communist organisations, personalities and persons.
