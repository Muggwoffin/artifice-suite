D.D.E.

Please see Pilkington's note at 100a and his minute 101. In view of the fact that knows nothing of the visit to Holland while has apparently read into it more than he was actually told, I do not feel that we can oppose the release of the LOEVENHEIMs on this account.

I would, however, like to call your attention to Pilkington's paragraph six because I feel that there is considerable force in the argument "once a Soviet agent always a Soviet agent". I do not feel that this point of view was brought out in your note at 96a.

If you are discussing conditions in connection with the release of the LOEVENHEIMs I consider it important not to draw any parallel between their case and those of the six Czech Communists. The Czechs were released partly at the request of the Czech Government and we agreed at the time that the release was made for political motives. The LOEVENHEIMs are enemy aliens and if we once draw any sort of parallel between the two I feel that the Home Office is likely to use this against us and press for the release, on the same conditions, of all enemy alien Communists.

Could I please have this file back as soon as you have finished with it?
