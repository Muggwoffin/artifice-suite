29.6.41

I should like to remember that the details given on 26th inst. were recollected from the memory only.

Traute Loewenheim, the divorced wife of Ernst (younger brother of Walter, the latter one is dominating his younger brother) gave the following details.

Hella L., wife of Walter, has asked the Soviet Ambassador Majsky some time after their internment for an interview to secure his intervention for their release; this interview was refused.

Hella L. has no political understanding at all.

Repeatedly Traute made various enquiries about the release application of Walter and Ernst through Miss Ellen Wilkinson, M.P., Minister for Pensions; furthermore through Werner Klatt a German of about 35 years of age who came to this country 2 1/2 years ago being up to that time engaged by the Headoffice of the I.G. Farben, one of the leading members of "Neu Beginnen" for many years, at present assistant of Mr. Seaton-Wattson and known to Löwenheims since about 8 years, a half-jew; furthermore through Erwin Schöttle, former Wurttemberg M.P. as member of the German Labour Party and now one of the leaders of "Neu Beginnen".

At present she is waiting for Col. Josiah Wedgwood, M.P., of whom she is hoping that he will ask a question about her relatives in the House of Commons.

Both brothers are well known to the Moscow authorities from the old files but most of their Soviet Russian friends are dead by now or were dismissed 10 years ago; she mentioned the name of Sinovjeff but not Radek.

The brothers, according to her, were expelled from the Communist Party in 1928.

They started their "Neu Beginnen" in 1932/33 under the name of "Miles".

Löwenheims left Germany for good in 1935 to England.

Since arriving in this country only Walter went one time to Holland.

Bruno Karthäuser since in this country usually spent his holidays in France.

Traute admits that their people made a mistake that they, up to their internment, claimed to be refugees and they only have registered themselves as refugees with the Jewish Refugee Committee in late 1939 or early 1940.
