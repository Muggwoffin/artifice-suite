I suppose you hardly noticed International Youth Day in London. In Moscow you could notice nothing else. All the streets were alive with glorious kids, most of them carrying banners, many of them dressed up, and all shouting just for the sheer joy of making a noise. They were a lovely crowd, ranging from kids of Marca's age to some who had long passed their youth. I took some pictures of them, and if they come out, I will send you copies. My photographing has been rather a failure up to the present. Very few have come out - I have either moved the camera or got the focus wrong. By the way, I loved those pictures you sent me of you and Clemens and Rath. Have you taken any others? Do send me some if you have.

Your holiday with Serner sounded so good. It made me feel almost jealous, cos I'm not going to have one this year. I'm saving up for next year, when I'm going to have a real bumper - Caucasus, Crimea and so forth. Of course, being in Moscow is a sort of holiday, particularly at the present moment when I have hardly any work to do. God, I've never done so little work in all my life. Its very demoralising, but at the moment I am enjoying the contrast. The damnable part is that I am not making enough use of my time. However, I shall pull myself together soon, when all this D.H. business is over. I have got to go off now to some sort of discussion.

Do write again soon, and tell me what is happening to you and all.

By the way, where are you living now? With the newly married ones? Give them my love, and the others when you see them.

Ever,
Rose
