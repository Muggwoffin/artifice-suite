A report in the "Zeitung" of 10.7.42. stated

"1936. In spite of the Gestapo the German Freedom Sender will be on the air every evening on wave 29,8."

The "German Freedom Sender", however, was in Spain and not in Germany! Transmissions ceased several days before the fall of Barcelone. Apart from this the Italian Communist emigrants also used the same sender; it would be hard to believe that the Italian Communists had their headquarters in Germany. The "Zeitung" continues:

"Prinz LOEWE NSTEIN's appeal to the Catholic Youth of Germany appears as an advertisement for food; other brochures are disguised as advertisements for baking powder, chess problems, town plans and even as Nazi literature."

LOEWENSTEIN's appeal was printed in Paris. This and the other disguised publications were printed by Willi MUENZENBERG, who was in charge of this sort of propaganda in Paris. How many of these printed pamphlets succeeded in reaching Germany is never mentioned.

The "Zeitung" reports further:

"Hitler had the 'Siegfried Line' built. An unusual photographed document shows the attitude of the workers used on its construction. Seven of them stand in a row, representatives of half a million strikers - and four of the seven refuse to give the Hitler greeting to the 'Fuehrer' and his generals as they pass by! What has happened to these nameless heroes?"

Why "strikers"? There was no strike. Why "nameless heroes"? There were and there are in Germany forced labourers, convicts, whom it is forbidden to use the Hitler greeting. The Jews are not allowed to use it, and in the prisons and concentration camps the Hitler greeting is only used among the guards. In general, however, there was no order that civilians should use this greeting, particularly when working.
