The Communist Party desired the destruction of imperialism, capitalist Germany; the Social-Democrats desired its preservation. That was the antithesis from which every later struggle developed. The Communist Party was drawn from groups that opposed the German Labour Movement. Nevertheless, neither the imperial policy of conquest and the world war, nor the consequent miserable condition of many millions of German workers, could stir them up to a definite revolutionary policy. The Communist Party struggled alone. Their leaders and thousands of their functionaries were murdered or imprisoned. The sacrificial offering could never be paid in full. The weakness and mistakes of the Communist Party in the last years before Hitler's accession to power were the result of the heavy sacrifices of the earlier post-war years.

From now on Federalisation implies a re-building of workers' organisations on Federal lines. Never again a central Trades Union, but communal industrial organisations instead.

To avoid Nazi hatred in Germany the work of cleansing the country must be carried out by Germans.

Every German, without exception, must have a share in the task of reparation, just as every German is responsible for the war and its consequences.

Reparation must be activated in the first place by objective and employment necessity, so that people in the invaded countries do not have to wait for decades for shelter and the commonest household utensil.

There must be no more provision for war. Maintenance of the severely wounded and the crippled is a matter for the community.

The tendency, already observable, among the guilty to hide themselves in religious communities etc must be countered. On the whole the German church, but particularly the protestant church, has supported Hitler's efforts. The occasional protests from ecclesiastical quarters have not touched the fundamental problems, but were in the nature of protests against restrictions on church rights.

Political mistakes, like military ones, avenge themselves from the moment they are committed. For this reason there can be no extenuating plea for incompetent leaders. They too must be held responsible for Hitler's Government of today.

If German opposition is not welded into an organisation now, it will be as powerless after the war as it was in 1918.
