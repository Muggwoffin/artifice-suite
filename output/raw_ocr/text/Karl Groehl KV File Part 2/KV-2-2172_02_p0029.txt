From: S. Retzlaw,
10 D. Belsize Gardens,
London. N.W.3.

To: Mr. P.A. Stearn,
35 West 75th Street,
New York City.

Date: 28th February, 1942.

CONFIDENTIAL

Letter in German.
Photostat taken.

"Dear Pitt,

With reference once again to your letter and to mine of February 19th. I am not clear what you mean by work. You can get a thousand times more work in U.S.A. than here. You would not get permission for the journey. But even if you did - you would be treated like a shoe-black. If you only knew how I have been treated here. Still you do know that I have neither a dinner-jacket nor a bank account. Anyone who hasn't a dress-suit nor a bank account is considered a swine here. People are rich and have pedigrees. In my business they are all stinking reactionaries. I should be happier if I were working in a factory in New York. You must know that when I was wandering about on the continent until I got to Lisbon, no one from here helped me. People are incredibly arrogant.

I am anxious to find out what you have written to the people. A letter from you must certainly be on its way. In spite of everything I am not pessimistic. Better days will come again. One must not let oneself get spiritually or physically demoralised but stick to one's purpose. We are not all boot-lickers. But only boot-lickers are loved. If I say a word here people threaten to take away my livelihood. Believe me, I have never led such a miserable existence as here. In spite of all this the day will come."
