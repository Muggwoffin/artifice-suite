From: S. Retzlaw,
10 D Belsize Park Gardens,
London. N.W.3.

To: Mr. H.G. Frueh,
Lindenweg 4,
Kilchberg bei Zurich.

Date: 2nd March, 1942.

CONFIDENTIAL

Written in German.

Dear Friend,

I am very pleased to have your letter of 9.2. which reached me very quickly. Many thanks for your greetings and those of your family which I return equally affectionately.

Unfortunately things are not quite as you write. Matters are by no means well with me. My hosts are not such "gentlemen" as the saying was in novels. You know what it is like with the bourgeoisie: if one receives a blow he has his own back on the first weaker person he can find. Thus it is with me because I have no money, i.e. because I am not rich; have no evening clothes, always refused a title formerly, for this reason they think they can be impertinent. This will end now. The war is developing as I foresaw it would, minds and hearts are becoming hardened, people are becoming spiteful, petty, sordid, etc.

Nevertheless your optimism is justified and I hope that you judge correctly when you write that the majority of the population is also optimistic so long as everything necessary is done. And it must be done. It takes a long time but will come in the end.
