The workers in question in the picture show no sign of truculence, but merely of curiosity. What is there heroic in that? Every inhabitant in England during the air bombardments showed more heroism without such a fuss being made. The "Zeitung" continues:

"The active part played by the refugees in the war industries of the Allied peoples is described as unassuming."

That is indeed a true saying, for the war effort of the refugees is, in fact, very modest.

In the "News Review" of 9.7.42. in an article about the Exhibition, there is an entirely misplaced attack on Lord Vansittart. The heroine of the article is "Minna", a brave woman "with little ambition to make headlines." That is modest. There were, namely, no headlines, because the work had so little effect that she might never have done it. It must be observed that until 1938 people who were not known to the police had very little difficulty in crossing the frontier into Czechoslovakia. Tens of thousands crossed the frontier and on Saturdays and Sundays there was very little control of the holidaymakers.

On 11.7.42. the "News Chronicle" said

"The resistance movement is growing daily. Socialists, Communists and Catholics, who once worked separately against the Nazis, have now joined forces. Over 200,000 of them are actively engaged within the Reich, printing and distributing illegal newsheets which are reaching at least 4,000,000 readers every month. A collection of original illegal newspapers circulated by these anti-Nazis, and photographs illustrating their work, are being shown at an exhibition arranged by the Free German League of Culture at 149 Regent Street, W."

There is nothing to prove the truth of any of these statements. The purpose of the exhibition is, however, clear: not only is money to be collected, but the English public are to be deceived as to the true situation in Germany, sympathy is to be aroused for a Germany which, unfortunately, has not existed for a long time. If it were true that 200,000 active workers had 4,000,000 readers every month, there would be certain results which, as yet, are not to be seen. To-day the R.A.F. carries on the work of distributing pamphlets, not the fictitious "200,000 active workers."

P.T.O.
