In the subsequent discussion, Rolf BADER thought it wrong to exaggerate certain events in Germany. Atrocities were committed by soldiers of all nations. He also deprecated propaganda which threatened the German people with war until they got rid of Hitler, as such terroristic tactics would only achieve the reverse of what they intended. There would be no revolution in Germany; and the only thing to do was to defeat Nazi Germany in battle.
RETZLAW replied that what the Germans had done was a thousand times worse than any atrocity propaganda made out. Also it was not right to impute atrocities to soldiers of all nations. British soldiers did not rob, rape or murder civilians. It was common knowledge that a brigadier of the British army had been degraded and cashiered because he struck a German prisoner with a stick.
Hans JAGER announced that on the following Sunday he would speak on "The Conservatives, the Army and the Hitler Regime". Afterwards there would be a lecture once a fortnight and study groups would be formed to deal with interesting problems. He said the time was come to replace words by constructive work, in contradistinction to the old parties, such as the SOPADE, which only wanted to restore the status quo so that its leading lights could have their old jobs back.
26 persons attended the meeting, including Bernhard MENNE, Otto LEHMANN-RUSSBUELDT, Heinrich ALLINA, Paul HUMPOLETZ, Paul PLURA, Emil GROSSHEIM, Günther BENJAMIN and Peter HOEFLER.
