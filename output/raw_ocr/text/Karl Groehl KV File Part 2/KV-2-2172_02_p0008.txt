I spoke to General Lakin about this matter again this morning and he put me on to Major Thornley. The latter told me that if RETZLAW was to be interviewed it must be conducted in German on very tough lines. It was clear, to me at any rate, that I was not the person to conduct an interview of this kind, and Thornley informed me that we had on one previous occasion supplied an officer who was apparently a past master at this type of interview. Discreet enquiries revealed that this officer was Meurig Evans from Camp 020! I got in touch with Stephens who told me that Meurig Evans was no longer with him, but that he would be prepared for Sampson to conduct the interview. It has now been arranged that Sampson shall go to an appointed address at 3.15 p.m. next Friday, the 20th March. He will there be given his riding orders and at 4 p.m. will interview RETZLAW.
