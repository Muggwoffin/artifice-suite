68, Redcliffe Square
London, S.W.10
July 13th 1944

To Mr. Stokes and Mr. Strauss M.P. (Sec.)
House of Commons
Westminster
London S.W.1.

Dear Sirs,

I read your questions put in the House of Commons yesterday about East Prussia and "Turn out the Nazis". In my opinion the problem of East Prussia (that drill ground for the German army) is not a point of controversy for real socialists. The "Manifesto" about it (and the Atlantic Charter) signed by German social democrats (this word does not necessarily imply "socialists" because these signatories are "Noskists" - the murderers of German revolutionaries) is de facto a pro-Nazi manifesto.

The case of "Turn out the Nazis" is similar. Ebert, the leader of the social democrats during and after the first world war said: "I hate the revolution like sin" an attitude which is even today that of many German social democrats.

Nothing comes from nothing. The social democratic refugees in this country and especially in the U.S.A. are objectively counter-revolutionaries. Their role as a government party in Germany was to screen re-armament and thus prepare the way to power for Hitler and his gang. This the social democrats did well.

If these ridiculous party officials like Vogel, Ollenhauer, Sering-Loewenthal, the semi-Nazis Stampfer, Schiff, Mrs. Schreiber etc. would live today in Germany they would be more pro-war than even their friend Tanner-Finland.

And what is done by the British Labour Party, Socialists Trade Union to help the development of a rise of the German people. Nothing, or even less. By supporting the reactionary German sozial democrats and trade-unions they suppress a real German revolutionary movement.

It is a fact - in my humble opinion - that in the endeavours to prevent a third world war and to re-educate the German people Lord Vansittart is doing more than the whole of the Labour-International included the British Labour Party and Trade Union Congress and the book by Lord Vansittart "Lessons of my life" is more instructive to the Germans than all the publications of the Labour Party and the books by Prof. Lasi and Brailsford.

I am really sorry that I had to write a letter like this one being on the German Labour Movement for more than 30 years but I feel so entitled to say something about it.

Yours faithfully,
signed S. Retzlaw
