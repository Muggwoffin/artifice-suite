I went by arrangement on March 20th to a flat in Hans Crescent at 3.15 p.m. to see an agent of S.O.E. The agent turned out to be a man called Karl GROEHL, alias Stanislas RETZLAW. The story given to me by Mrs. Holmes is as follows.

He worked as an agent in Paris and ran a group of agents in Switzerland. When the Germans invaded France he disappeared, although arrangements could have been made to bring him to this country. He obtained Polish papers under the name Stanislas RETZLAW at some place in Unoccupied France and eventually arrived at Lisbon. He came to this country with British assistance in about October, 1940, and has since been receiving a monthly wage of £25.

On February 7th, 1942, RETZLAW wrote a subversive letter to the agents in Switzerland. He refused to send another letter correcting the information he had given and his pay was, therefore, stopped.

Since then, it has been reported by an Austrian that RETZLAW had said he was in the British Intelligence Service and controlled connections with Germany and Austria.

I was asked to point out to him the seriousness of this offence, which might easily endanger his contacts in Switzerland. I then saw RETZLAW and had a conversation with him in German, as he speaks very little English. I gave him a severe warning that the result of such an action might easily bring about his internment and the stoppage of all his future activities until the end of the war. He protested that he had not informed anyone of his connection with the British Intelligence Service, but said this was the conclusion to which a number of people who knew him might easily come. He was very unwilling to write a further letter to Switzerland on the grounds that his monthly salary had been made conditional upon such a letter and that he was very unwilling to act under compulsion. He was eventually induced to write the letter on the spot and it was considered to be satisfactory.

GS/FHL.
21.3.42.
