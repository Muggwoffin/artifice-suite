Dear Hig,

I have been brooding over your letter three days before I could bring myself to answer it. Even now, I don't know what to say. There is one thing you want to hear, and I can't say it. I would have given anything for this not to have happened. I now feel I must have encouraged you in all sorts of ways, but it never occurred to my dull-witted brain that such a thing could happen to you as far as I was concerned. I really mean this, Hig. — God knows I'm very just saying the conventional thing — I'm not good enough all that. We were such comrades, there it seemed to begin and end.
