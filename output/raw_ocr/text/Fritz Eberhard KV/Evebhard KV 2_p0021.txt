Interview with SOURCE: 13.1.42.

The two members of ISK whom [redacted] characterised in his report on EICHLER as anti-British are Hilde MONTE and Helmut von RAUSCHENPLAT, both of whom have recently left the ISK. Kurt does not know why, but suspects that it is on political grounds.

[redacted] ascertained the real opinions of these people when he met them at a party in 1939. On that occasion the conversation turned to Eichler's Publishing Firm in Paris, Editions Internationale and [redacted] remarked that it had printed a lot of good books and that it was a pity it spoilt its reputation by publishing Eichler's book. MONTE and RAUSCHENPLAT thereupon protested that this was the best book the firm had published and a discussion took place which became quite acrimonious.

Since that time these two persons have jointly written a book entitled "How to conquer Hitler" which was published by Jarrolds in 1940. It deals with the economic situation in Germany mainly. [Hilde MONTE] has a mysterious job which [redacted] thinks may be under the British Government. At any rate she is in London for regular periods and then goes off travelling for regular periods. He has an idea it is something connected with propaganda but cannot say anything further about this.

He told me that he also thought RAUSCHENPLAT had some sort of job which may be under the Government. He is indignant that these two people who have shown themselves anti-British should possibly be employed by the Government.

With regard to their anti-British sentiments, he says that both are ardent francophiles and hold that British interference in continental affairs is responsible for all Europe's troubles. On the other hand, [redacted] wishes to specify that this does not mean they should be regarded as pro-German. On the contrary, he regards them both as 100% anti-Nazi.

C. W. Sykes.
